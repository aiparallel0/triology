\begin{abstract}
\boldmath
\linespread{1}\selectfont
Document-AI systems frequently extract itemized fields from receipts, where the ``total'' field serves as the highest-value target. Errors in extracting this specific field can severely propagate into downstream processes like tax filings, expense reports, and financial audits. In standard practice, the softmax layers in Document-AI models often output miscalibrated predictions~\cite{guo2017calibration}, failing to detect these errors in isolation. To remedy this issue, we study sigma-verifier, a model-external validation layer that frames verification as a 0/1-knapsack problem, checking whether any subset of extracted itemized lines sums exactly to the predicted total. Arithmetic cross-checking is established industrial practice; to our knowledge its formalisation as a softmax-orthogonal selective-prediction signal, and its systematic evaluation, are not. We evaluated the impact of the sigma-verifier, on receipt-parsing systems utilizing two prominent architectures Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} and LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} across various benchmark databases. \section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\emph{Receipt key-information extraction} (KIE) means: a model reads a
receipt image and outputs named fields: vendor, date, taxes, and
especially the \emph{total}. The outputs flow directly into
tax filings, expense pipelines, audit logs, and
bookkeeping. A silently wrong
\emph{total} is not recoverable downstream; commercial vendors
(Expensify, QuickBooks, SAP Concur) process millions of
receipts per day and a wrong total at scale translates into major failure. The standard mitigation is \emph{selective
prediction}~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,elyaniv2010foundations}: let
the model defer (say ``I'm not sure'') on inputs it cannot reliably
handle. Every deployment needs a signal to drive the defer-versus-accept
decision.

\noindent\textbf{The longstanding default and its flaw.} For the modern
era of neural KIE the only such signal in this setting has been
\emph{softmax confidence}: when a model produces an answer one token
at a time, softmax converts its internal scores into probabilities
that sum to $1$ at each token position. The geometric mean of the
probabilities the model assigned to the tokens it actually emitted is
the standard ``how sure am I''
number~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,vashurin2025benchmark,
fadeeva2023polygraph}. The flaw is structural: softmax is a property
of the \emph{generator itself}, so a model that hallucinates a
probable total often does so confidently. Industrial OCR pipelines
have long applied post-detection arithmetic cross-checks; what has been
missing is a peer-reviewed formalisation of such a check as an
independent (model-internal-free) verification signal with paired
statistical analysis against softmax.

\noindent\textbf{Our contribution.} We propose
$\sigma$\footnote{Throughout, $\sigma$ names the verifier
defined below, not the standard-deviation symbol.} as that independent
signal: it accepts the model's predicted total when some subset of the
monetary amounts visible on the receipt sums to it within tolerance
(Section~\ref{sec:method}). The witness exists by
construction on well-formed receipts: the total \emph{is} a sum of
visible amounts. The verifier is a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic
program (DP), with measured sub-millisecond $p_{99}$ latency
(Section~\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\begin{chgblock}
\noindent\textbf{The general principle.} \emph{When a task's output admits a
checkable structural constraint, a deterministic verifier of that constraint
is a confidence signal statistically orthogonal to the model's own softmax,
because its errors are governed by document structure rather than decoder
logits.} Receipts are a clean instantiation; the construction applies
wherever a structural invariant is checkable --- balancing tables, schemas
with referential constraints, code with type checks, logic with a solver.
\end{chgblock}

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~$\sigma$, to our knowledge the
first \emph{peer-reviewed formalisation of structural (arithmetic)

instantiated on receipt KIE (Section~\ref{sec:method}). (ii)~Empirical validation on a pooled

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Selective prediction has a long history~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,
elyaniv2010foundations,jaeger2024augrc}; for sequence-to-sequence
generators (such as Donut) the standard signals are derived from the
generator's own token-level probabilities~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,
vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph} or from sample-based
semantic divergence~\cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024semantic}.
$\sigma$ is not a different
softmax aggregator but a different \emph{signal} that does not read
the model at all. \chgt{This is also why softmax is the only baseline here:
Monte-Carlo dropout, deep ensembles and semantic entropy are all computed from
the model's internals, so each inherits the failure mode $\sigma$ exists to
escape --- a model that hallucinates a probable total is confident under all of
them. The comparison we owe, and do not have, is a second \emph{model-external}
verifier; we know of none published for this task.}

\noindent\textbf{Why arithmetic checking is already industrial practice.}
Arithmetic cross-checks of receipt totals are common engineering practice ---
expense-management software has used them for at least a decade --- because
the check is closed-form, needs no model, and catches precisely the errors an
operator pays for later. What is missing is their analysis as a
selective-prediction signal (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\noindent\textbf{Program-level verification.} Verification of generative
outputs has appeared in narrower settings (code-execution validation,
faithful chain-of-thought); $\sigma$ instantiates that spirit for receipt
KIE, where the target is closed-form arithmetic over visible quantities.

We evaluate two architectures. Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} is a
\emph{generative} vision-encoder-decoder producing structured JSON;
LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} is an \emph{encoder-only}
token-classifier (tagging each token with a class such as
Total_value). Structure-aware
verification has appeared for table
reconstruction~\cite{nassar2022tableformer}; what is new here is
formalising an arithmetic check as a softmax-orthogonal selective
predictor on fixed off-the-shelf KIE models, with paired statistical
analysis across two architectures. Conformal
prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle} gives
calibrated coverage but no verifier-style precision decision rule; the two are
complementary and stackable (conformal calibrates from past
prediction-truth pairs; $\sigma$ verifies one prediction against the
document's own arithmetic).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Problem.} Given a receipt image $x$, a KIE model emits a
predicted total $\pi(x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$. Let $y(x)$ be the
gold total. A selective predictor is a binary decision rule
$g:x\mapsto\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}$ with
\emph{coverage} $C(g)=\Pr[g{=}\text{accept}]$ (how often it doesn't
defer) and \emph{precision}
$P(g)=\Pr(\pi{=}y\mid g{=}\text{accept})$ (how often it's right when
it doesn't defer).

\noindent\textbf{The $\sigma$ decision rule.} Let $M(x)=\{m_1,\dots,m_n\}$ be the
multiset of monetary amounts extracted from $x$. Fix
$k_{\min}\in\{1,2\}$ (the smallest subset size we'll accept as a
witness; without this, a single line item that happens to equal the
total would always pass) and tolerance $\varepsilon\geq 0$ (in
currency units). Define the reachable set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
T(M)=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}m_i\,\Big|\,
   S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\,|S|\geq k_{\min}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff $\inf_{t\in T(M)}|t-\pi|\leq\varepsilon$:
some subset of receipt amounts sums to the predicted total within
$\varepsilon$. $T(M)$ is computed by a textbook $0/1$-knapsack
DP. Amounts are stored as integer cents to avoid floating-point
surprises. Measured on CPU the per-receipt DP has median
\latMedian~\latUnit{} and $p_{99}$ of \latPNineNine~\latUnit{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}).

\noindent\textbf{Cardinality guard.} The right $k_{\min}$ depends on
whether the total typically appears on the receipt as a labelled
line. On labelled-amount corpora the labelled line is part of $M$ and
a singleton witness $\pi{=}m_j$ is a coincidence rather than the
intended structural match, so we set $k_{\min}{=}2$ to exclude it; on
OCR-derived corpora the OCR step often surfaces the labelled total
itself, and the singleton witness is then legitimate, so $k_{\min}{=}1$
helps on both axes (Table~\ref{tab:guard}). The default in this paper
is $k_{\min}{=}2$ on CORD and WildReceipt, $k_{\min}{=}1$ on SROIE.
This is selected without labels: $k_{\min}{=}1$ iff the extractor
surfaces a single amount equal to $\pi$ (within $\varepsilon$) on more
than a small fraction $\theta$ of receipts, an unlabelled, computable
property of the $(\pi, M)$ stream alone (we use $\theta{=}0.30$; the
three corpora separate cleanly at $0.07$/$0.05$/$0.41$ for
CORD/WildReceipt/SROIE, so the rule is insensitive to $\theta$ over
$[0.1,0.4]$). The guard is therefore a deployment-time decision rule,
not a label-tuned hyperparameter.

\noindent\textbf{Amount extraction $\tau$.} On labelled-amount corpora
(CORD), $M$ comes directly from the per-field JSON. On OCR-derived
corpora (SROIE), we use a multi-candidate extractor over the OCR text:
when a line is ambiguous (e.g.\ tax rate vs.\ tax amount), we keep
both interpretations as candidates $M_k$ and accept $\pi$ if any
candidate yields a witness ($3.12$ candidates per receipt on average).
On WildReceipt (encoder-only), $M$ comes from the per-token
Prod_price_value annotations.

\noindent\textbf{Composition with softmax.} Let $s(x)$ be the softmax
confidence. For a coverage budget $c$, the softmax decision rule
accepts the top-$c$ fraction of receipts by $s$. We compose at
per-corpus matched coverage: $c$ equal to $\sigma$'s coverage on that
corpus. The combination of the two decision rules,
$\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$, accepts iff both fire. We refer to any
such accept/defer decision rule, $\sigma$, softmax, or their
combination, as a \emph{gate}.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Datasets.} We use three public receipt benchmarks,
chosen to span the labelling regimes that matter in practice.
\textbf{CORD-v2}~\cite{park2019cord} test+validation ($n{=}200$,
labelled per-field amounts; CORD-v2 \emph{train} is the Donut
fine-tuning split and is excluded to keep the evaluation
leakage-free); \textbf{SROIE Task-3}~\cite{huang2019sroie} canonical
($n{=}347$, OCR-derived amounts); \textbf{WildReceipt}
\cite{sun2021wildreceipt} test ($n{=}472$, per-token annotations).
Pooled $n{=}\poolN{}$.

\noindent\textbf{AI Models.} We use Donut~\cite{kim2022donut}
fine-tuned on CORD-v2 and on SROIE, and
LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} fine-tuned on WildReceipt; all
inference-only.

\noindent\textbf{Settings.} $\varepsilon{=}0.02$ throughout;
$k_{\min}$ follows the label-free cardinality-guard rule of
Section~\ref{sec:method} ($k_{\min}{=}2$ on CORD and WildReceipt,
$k_{\min}{=}1$ on SROIE). The fixed-$k_{\min}$ sweep of
Table~\ref{tab:guard} is the only departure, and is explicitly an
ablation of that rule.

Table~\ref{tab:headline-pooled} reports pooled results across all
$1{,}019$ receipts. The key finding is that the combination of
$\sigma$ and softmax is the only configuration that clears the
$0.95$ precision floor required for fully automated processing,

accepts only $18.1\%$ of receipts, compared to $37.9\%$ for either

Whether this tradeoff is worthwhile depends on the deployment
context: it is favorable when the cost of a silently wrong total,
one that propagates unchallenged into tax filings or audit logs,
outweighs the cost of additional human review, which holds in
high-stakes financial pipelines but not in low-stakes bulk capture.
Quantitatively, with a human review costing $\rho$ inference passes
($\rho\in[10^2,10^3]$) and a silent wrong total costing $\kappa$,
moving from softmax-matched ($c{=}0.379$, $p{=}0.953$) to the
combination ($c{=}0.181$, $p{=}0.989$) is favourable whenever
$\kappa\gtrsim 13\rho$: a condition, not a universal claim.

maximise the automated fraction, the matched-coverage softmax rule accepts the

Table~\ref{tab:mcnemar} addresses a critical question: do $\sigma$
and softmax tend to fail on the same receipts, or on different ones?
If they failed together, combining them would offer little benefit:
you would simply be applying two filters that agree with each other
most of the time.

The McNemar test answers this by counting two types of disagreement.
$b$ counts receipts where $\sigma$ is correct but softmax is not,
mistakes that $\sigma$ catches but softmax misses. $c$ counts the
reverse, receipts where softmax is correct but $\sigma$ is not. If
$b$ and $c$ are roughly equal, neither signal is consistently better
than the other; they are simply catching different mistakes, which
is precisely what makes combining them powerful.

Across all three datasets the counts are strikingly balanced. On
WildReceipt, $b$ and $c$ are exactly equal at $91$, meaning $\sigma$
and softmax each rescue an identical number of receipts that the
other misses. The high $p$-values across all datasets, all above
$0.6$, are consistent with no detectable asymmetry in either
direction. This is the key evidence that the two signals are
independent: they fail on different receipts, so requiring both to
agree filters out errors that neither could catch alone. Strictly,
McNemar non-rejection is absence of evidence for asymmetry rather
than positive evidence of error-decorrelation; the direct
correlation-on-errors test (Section~\ref{sec:results-control})
supplies the latter.

\subsection{\chgi{RQ3: Is the Gain Genuine, or Generic Coverage Shrink?}}
\label{sec:results-control}

Combining any two better-than-random decision rules with imperfectly
correlated errors raises precision and lowers coverage; is the
headline just that generic effect? We re-analyse the same per-receipt
accept/correct vectors (no inference). Define the lift
$L=P(\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax})-P(\text{softmax at matched
coverage})$ and compare it, at identical coverage, against two
controls: (A)~a \emph{random} decision rule accepting a uniform
random subset of size $|\sigma{=}1|$ (no information; pure
coverage-shrink), and (B)~a \emph{correlated} decision rule
$\text{softmax}'=\text{softmax}+\text{noise}$, noise-calibrated so
$\text{softmax}'$ has $\sigma$-equal standalone precision (a
softmax-built, hence non-independent, decision rule of equal
quality). We test
the difference \emph{directly} with a paired bootstrap ($B{=}4000$):
each resample recomputes $L_\sigma$ and both control lifts on the
\emph{same} resample and takes the paired difference.
Pooled, $\sigma$'s lift exceeds the random decision rule by
$\orthDrand$ (95\,\% CI $[\orthDrandLo,\orthDrandHi]$, one-sided
$p{=}\orthDrandP$) and the correlated softmax-derived decision rule by
$\orthDcorr$
($[\orthDcorrLo,\orthDcorrHi]$, $p{=}\orthDcorrP$): significant against
\emph{both}. As a confound-free check we also measure error
decorrelation directly, within the softmax-accepted set: whether
$\sigma$ rejects precisely the softmax-wrong receipts, which gives
$\phi{=}\orthPhi$ (permutation $p{=}\orthPhiP$): positive evidence of
orthogonality with no coverage-shrink or combination-arithmetic
confound, which the paired-McNemar absence of asymmetry
(Section~\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}) only implies indirectly.
The pooled verdict is \orthEarned, bounded by two honest caveats.
First, the pooled effect is \emph{small} ($\orthDrand$/$\orthDcorr$
precision lift); the CORD evaluation uses only its test+validation
split ($n{=}200$, the Donut fine-tuning \emph{train} split
excluded), so no result is inflated by leakage. Second, it does
\emph{not} hold per corpus (Section~\ref{sec:results-percorpus-rigor}).
We therefore claim a \emph{small, pooled,
statistically-significant} orthogonality and explicitly not a
per-corpus or large one.

\smallskip
\label{sec:results-percorpus-rigor}
\noindent\textbf{Per-corpus rigor.} Exact small-sample analysis of
the leakage-free combination cells (CORD $54/55$, SROIE $15/15$,
WildReceipt $113/114$). Lower bounds agree across estimators
(Clopper--Pearson, Jeffreys, Wilson): CORD $\rgCordWil$, SROIE
$\rgSroieWil$, WildReceipt $\rgWrWil$; receipt-weighted pool
$182/184$ (Wilson LB $\rgPoolWil$), leave-one-corpus-out worst
$\rgLocoMinWil$. A one-sided exact-binomial TOST (H$_0$:
$p\le0.95$) clears the floor \emph{individually only on WildReceipt}
($p=\rgTostWrP$); CORD ($p=\rgTostCordP$) and SROIE
($p=\rgTostSroieP$) do \emph{not} pass (consistent with $>0.95$ but
underpowered), while the pool does ($p=\rgTostPoolP$). A
within-corpus permutation test of $\sigma$'s orthogonal contribution
gives combined Stouffer $p=\rgAFiveStouffer$ and Fisher
$p=\rgAFiveFisher$: decorrelation holds within every corpus, not
only pooled.

\subsection{\chgi{RQ4: Does It Beat Matched-Coverage Softmax?}}
\label{sec:results-riskcov}

Statistical orthogonality is necessary but not sufficient for
deployment value; does the $\sigma$ decision rule beat simply
thresholding softmax? We answer with a matched-coverage risk--coverage comparison
(no inference): at each coverage, softmax-alone versus the $\sigma$
decision rule at the \emph{same} achieved coverage, integrated to an
AURC~\cite{geifman2019aurc} (lower better). Pooled, AURC is
$\rcAURCsoft$ (softmax-alone) versus $\rcAURCsig$ ($\sigma$ decision
rule), a paired-bootstrap difference of $\rcAURCd$ (95\,\% CI
$[\rcAURCdLo,\rcAURCdHi]$, one-sided $p{=}\rcAURCp$): the interval
excludes zero, so at matched coverage the $\sigma$ decision rule
significantly lowers AURC; verdict \rcEarned. We do not over-read
this: the absolute difference is small and the lift costs coverage,
so we report it as a modest, statistically resolved operational gain,
concentrated where base error is non-trivial (WildReceipt).

\subsection{\chgi{RQ5: When Does Each Signal Win, and Why?}}
\label{sec:results-regime}

Single-rule $\sigma$ precision is regime-mechanistic: on CORD (clean
per-field labels) $\sigma$ beats softmax by $3.0$ points ($0.990$
vs.\ $0.960$ at matched coverage); on WildReceipt (per-token
annotations) the two decision rules tie at $0.953$; on SROIE
(OCR-derived $M$) $\sigma$ trails ($0.863$ vs.\ $0.945$). We
hypothesise the SROIE loss is consistent with upstream OCR rather
than the verifier: digit confusions and phantom amounts can inflate
$T(M)$ with coincidental witnesses. \chgt{Testing this on the extracted
amounts: of the $73$ receipts $\sigma$ accepts on SROIE, the $10$ it accepts
\emph{wrongly} carry more extracted amounts than the $63$ it accepts correctly
($6.7$ vs $4.3$; one-sided permutation test, $B{=}20{,}000$, $p{=}0.004$), with
larger reachable sets in the same direction though not significantly ($708$ vs
$209$, $p{=}0.14$); candidate interpretations per line show no difference
($3.30$ vs $3.33$, $p{=}0.57$). The mechanism is how many amounts OCR
surfaces, not how many ways each line is read. With $10$ false accepts this
supports the account rather than settling it.}

Table~\ref{tab:guard} isolates the effect of the cardinality guard
on SROIE, the dataset where OCR often surfaces the total itself as a
clean labelled line. When the guard is off ($k_{\min}{=}1$), a single
line item matching the predicted total is accepted as sufficient
evidence. When the guard is on ($k_{\min}{=}2$), at least two line
items must sum to the total. On SROIE, turning the guard off is
better on both axes: coverage rises from $2.9\%$ to $16.1\%$ and precision
rises from $0.900$ to $0.929$. This is because on OCR-derived data
the total genuinely does appear as a single clean line, so the
singleton match is legitimate rather than coincidental. Forcing two
items to sum to the total unnecessarily rejects valid receipts and
gains nothing in precision. This ablation fixes $k_{\min}$ with the
single-scalar extractor, so its SROIE accept set differs from the
multi-candidate headline of Table~\ref{tab:headline-pooled}; it is a
different extractor configuration, not a conflicting result.

Table~\ref{tab:robust} tests how well the arithmetic check holds up
when receipt data is deliberately corrupted. We inject increasing
proportions of false line item amounts, from $0\%$ up to $40\%$, and
measure the impact on both coverage and precision across $10$ random
seeds. The results show a clear and reassuring pattern: as noise
increases, coverage drops steadily (from $26.7\%$ down to $9.1\%$),
but precision remains stable throughout, staying between $0.949$ and
$0.980$ at every noise level. This means the arithmetic check becomes
more selective under noisy conditions, it accepts fewer receipts, but
the receipts it does accept remain reliably correct. It degrades
gracefully rather than catastrophically. \chgi{Intervals in
Table~\ref{tab:robust} are bootstrap $95\%$ over the ten seeds.}

The tolerance sweep
$\varepsilon \in \{0.01, 0.02, \dots, 5.0\}$ on CORD returns identical
(coverage, precision) at every value, reflecting sparsity of the
reachable set: adjacent reachable subset sums on CORD are spaced by
more than $\$5.00$.

\noindent\textbf{Latency.} Fig.~\ref{fig:latency} reports the
standalone processing time of the arithmetic check. The median time
per receipt is $4.07$ microseconds, with $99\%$ of receipts processed
in under $312$ microseconds and the slowest taking $715$
microseconds. These are extremely fast: for context, the AI model
inference that produces the predicted total takes hundreds of
milliseconds on a GPU, meaning the arithmetic check adds negligible
overhead to the overall pipeline. It is fast enough to run on every
receipt in any production setting without any concern about speed; we
do not, however, measure the end-to-end ratio on this CPU-only host.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Where $\sigma$ works, and why combining helps.}
Where $M$ comes from reliable annotations $\sigma$ beats or ties
softmax; SROIE is the sole inversion regime, attributed to
OCR-derived $M$ (a hypothesis; the hand-OCR-corrected subsample test
is future work). Combining helps because orthogonality needs only
errors on different inputs, not dominance, and the gain survives
swapping Donut for LayoutLMv3, so $\sigma$ is signal-orthogonal to
softmax across architectures.

\noindent\textbf{What $\sigma$ cannot do.} (i)~$\sigma$ requires
arithmetic closure of the target field; totals, subtotals, and taxes,
not vendor/date/address. (ii)~$\varepsilon{=}0.02$ assumes
integer-cent inputs; fractional-cent rounding jurisdictions may need
a different sweep. (iii)~$\sigma$ assumes $\pi$ is a parsed numeric
scalar; format-hallucinated outputs require an upstream normaliser we
do not benchmark. (iv)~In the OCR-derived regime $\sigma$ is
valuable only in combination, not as a single decision rule
(Section~\ref{sec:results-regime}).

\noindent\textbf{Statistical scope.} The headline is a per-corpus
claim; the pooled $n{=}\poolN{}$ figure is carried by WildReceipt
($\wrIntA{}$ of $\poolIntA{}$ combined-rule accepts) and is presented
as secondary. The SROIE combined-rule cell ($n{=}15$) supports only
a consistency statement. The cardinality-guard inversion is measured
directly only on SROIE; the CORD-side claim is structural. We
evaluate one field across two backbones and three corpora;
multi-field $\sigma$ is journal work\chgt{: verifying line items, taxes and
total as one constraint system should tighten the witness, and using $\sigma$
as a training-time filter rather than only an inference-time gate would let the
structural signal shape the model instead of only auditing it. Neither is
evaluated here}.

\noindent\textbf{Scope of novelty.} Arithmetic cross-checks are
long-standing engineering practice; our contribution is their
peer-reviewed formalisation as a softmax-orthogonal selective
predictor with the paired and control statistics of
Sections~\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}--\ref{sec:results-control}, not
the idea of checking arithmetic. This is an applied contribution; we
claim no methodological novelty in the verifier itself.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented the sigma-verifier, a model-agnostic validation layer that directly evaluates a document by checking whether any subset of extracted itemized lines sums to the predicted total. We evaluated its performance across two document AI architectures and three receipt databases.

Our results show that combining the sigma-verifier with the model's softmax confidence reduces the silent-wrong-total rate among the receipts it accepts. Pooled across the three corpora it falls from 4.7\% to 1.1\%, an $\approx$77\% relative reduction, roughly four-fold fewer audit-exposing failures, at the coverage cost set out above, and the pooled lift is individually significant only on WildReceipt. Used as an independent validation layer under that coverage trade, the sigma-verifier offers structural benefits for high-stakes financial reporting and downstream auditing systems.
