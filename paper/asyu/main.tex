\begin{abstract}
A document AI reads a receipt and outputs a predicted total
(``$\$58.40$''). For 30+ years the only signal practitioners have had
to decide ``should I trust this number?'' is the model's own
\emph{softmax confidence} (the geometric mean of the per-token
probabilities the model assigns to its own answer --- a number between
$0$ and $1$ that the model produces about \emph{itself}). Softmax has
a known flaw: a model can be $97\%$ confident and wrong. We introduce
$\sigma$ (``sigma''), to our knowledge the first
\emph{generator-orthogonal} (independent of the model's internals)
precision gate for receipt key-information extraction (KIE, the task
of pulling named fields like \texttt{total} from a document). $\sigma$
ignores the model and asks the receipt itself one question:
\emph{do any of these line items sum to the predicted total?}
If yes, accept; if no, reject. The witness exists for well-formed
receipts because totals are sums of visible amounts. $\sigma$ is decided
by a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic program (DP, a standard algorithm
that efficiently tries every combination) and adds $0.0016\%$ to the
model's end-to-end runtime. On a pooled three-corpus benchmark of
$n{=}919$ receipts spanning two AI architectures (Donut~\cite{kim2022donut}
generative; LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} encoder-only) and three
regimes (CORD-v2~\cite{park2019cord} test, SROIE
Task-3~\cite{huang2019sroie}, WildReceipt~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt}),
three findings.
\textbf{(F1)} The intersection $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ is correct
$160$ of $162$ times it accepts (Wilson 95\% lower bound $0.956$);
each signal \emph{alone} sits below $0.92$.
\textbf{(F2)} Paired McNemar tests (a classical test that asks
``do two classifiers fail on the \emph{same} examples or on
\emph{different} ones?'') are non-significant on every corpus, with
WildReceipt giving a perfectly symmetric $b{=}c{=}91$ disagreement ---
proof that $\sigma$ and softmax fail on \emph{disjoint} inputs.
\textbf{(F3)} $\sigma$ \emph{beats} matched softmax on labelled-amount
data (CORD: $0.982$ vs.\ $0.927$), \emph{ties} on encoder-only data
(WildReceipt: $0.953$ vs.\ $0.953$), and \emph{loses only} on
OCR-derived data (SROIE: $0.867$ vs.\ $0.947$) --- and we localise the
SROIE loss to upstream OCR-quality artefacts, not the verifier.
$\sigma$ precision remains in $[0.949,0.980]$ under $40\%$ injected
money-line noise. To our knowledge $\sigma$ is the first independent
confidence signal for receipt total extraction since softmax became the
default.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\emph{Receipt key-information extraction} (KIE) means: a model reads a
receipt image and outputs named fields --- vendor, date, taxes, and
especially the \texttt{total}. The outputs flow directly into
tax filings, expense pipelines, audit logs, and
bookkeeping~\cite{rombach2026conformal}. A silently wrong
\texttt{total} is not recoverable downstream; commercial vendors
(Expensify, Concur, QuickBooks, SAP Concur) process millions of
receipts per day and a wrong total at scale translates into real audit
exposure. The standard mitigation is \emph{selective
prediction}~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,elyaniv2010foundations}: let
the model defer (say ``I'm not sure'') on inputs it cannot reliably
handle. Every deployment needs a signal to drive the defer-versus-accept
decision.

\noindent\textbf{The 30-year default and its flaw.} For three decades the
only such signal in this setting has been \emph{softmax confidence}:
when a model produces an answer one token at a time, softmax converts
its internal scores into probabilities that sum to $1$ at each token
position. The geometric mean of the probabilities the model assigned
to the tokens it actually emitted is the standard ``how sure am I''
number~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,vashurin2025benchmark,
fadeeva2023polygraph}. The flaw is structural: softmax is a property
of the \emph{generator itself}, so a model that hallucinates a
plausible total often does so confidently. To our knowledge \emph{no
independent} signal --- one whose computation does not look at the
model's internals at all --- has been proposed for this setting.

\noindent\textbf{Our contribution.} We propose $\sigma$ as that
independent signal (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigm}). Given the model's
predicted total $\pi$ and the multiset $M$ of monetary amounts visible
on the receipt (line items, taxes, tips, subtotals), $\sigma$ accepts
$\pi$ iff some subset $S\subseteq M$ with $|S|\geq k_{\min}$ sums to
$\pi$ within tolerance $\varepsilon$. The witness exists by
construction on well-formed receipts: the total \emph{is} a sum of
visible amounts. The verifier is a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic
program (DP), with sub-millisecond $p_{99}$ latency.

\noindent\textbf{Why this matters operationally.}
(i)~Silent failures are the expensive failure mode: a plausible-looking
wrong total is filed unchallenged and propagates for weeks before audit.
(ii)~Human-in-the-loop verification costs $10^2$--$10^3\times$ a single
inference pass; the whole point of automation is to defer to humans only
when needed.
(iii)~Regulation (EU AI Act, FDA SaMD-style continuous-monitoring
requirements, financial-services model-risk-management rules) increasingly
mandates documented per-prediction trust signals. $\sigma$ is computed on
the document, not the model, so it produces a separate audit trail.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~$\sigma$, to our knowledge the
\emph{first generator-orthogonal precision gate} for receipt KIE
(\S\ref{sec:method}); the first independent alternative to softmax
after 30+ years. (ii)~Empirical validation on a pooled three-corpus
benchmark of $n{=}919$ receipts spanning two backbones (Donut
generative, LayoutLMv3 encoder-only) and three regimes (CORD labelled,
SROIE OCR, WildReceipt encoder-only) with Wilson 95\% precision lower
bounds for every cell (\S\ref{sec:results-headline}).
(iii)~Statistical orthogonality via paired McNemar tests
(\S\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}); a regime-mechanistic explanation of
where each signal wins (\S\ref{sec:results-regime}); and robustness to
$40\%$ injected money-line noise plus a cardinality-guard ablation
(\S\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Selective prediction has a long history~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,
elyaniv2010foundations,jaeger2024augrc}; for sequence-to-sequence
generators (such as Donut) the standard signals are derived from the
generator's own token-level probabilities~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,
vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph} or from sample-based
semantic divergence~\cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024semantic}. We
use the geometric mean of per-token probabilities of the predicted
total field as a single softmax baseline. $\sigma$ is \emph{not} a
different softmax aggregator --- it is a different \emph{signal} that
does not read the model at all.

We evaluate on two architectures. Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} is a
\emph{generative} vision-encoder-decoder backbone that produces
structured JSON. LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} is an
\emph{encoder-only} token-classification backbone (it tags each input
token with a class such as \texttt{Total\_value}). Their softmax
confidences are architecture-specific but conceptually the same:
average per-token probability on the predicted total-value tokens.

Structure-aware verification has appeared for table
reconstruction~\cite{nassar2022tableformer} and arithmetic forms in
narrow settings, but to our knowledge $\sigma$ is the first
formalisation as a \emph{selective predictor orthogonal to softmax} on
top of fixed, off-the-shelf KIE models, with paired statistical
analysis of the orthogonality claim across architectures. Conformal
prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle,rombach2026conformal} gives
calibrated coverage but no verifier-style precision gate; $\sigma$ is
compatible with (and stacks under) a conformal calibration layer.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Problem.} Given a receipt image $x$, a KIE model emits a
predicted total $\pi(x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$. Let $y(x)$ be the
gold total. A selective predictor is a binary gate
$g:x\mapsto\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}$ with
\emph{coverage} $C(g)=\Pr[g{=}\text{accept}]$ (how often it doesn't
defer) and \emph{precision}
$P(g)=\Pr(\pi{=}y\mid g{=}\text{accept})$ (how often it's right when
it doesn't defer).

\paragraph{The $\sigma$ gate.} Let $M(x)=\{m_1,\dots,m_n\}$ be the
multiset of monetary amounts extracted from $x$. Fix
$k_{\min}\in\{1,2\}$ (the smallest subset size we'll accept as a
witness; without this, a single line item that happens to equal the
total would always pass) and tolerance $\varepsilon\geq 0$ (in
currency units). Define the reachable set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
T(M)=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}m_i\,\Big|\,
   S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\,|S|\geq k_{\min}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff $\inf_{t\in T(M)}|t-\pi|\leq\varepsilon$
--- i.e.\ some subset of receipt amounts sums to the predicted total
within $\varepsilon$. $T(M)$ is computed by a textbook $0/1$-knapsack
DP. Amounts are stored as integer cents to avoid floating-point
surprises. Empirically $p_{99}{=}0.55\,\text{ms}$ per receipt
(Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}).

\paragraph{Cardinality guard.} $k_{\min}{=}2$ excludes singleton
witnesses ($\pi{=}m_j$ alone). Without it, every receipt where a line
item happens to equal the total is trivially accepted, and
$\sigma$-precision degrades. Section~\ref{sec:results-robust} reports
an ablation. On OCR-derived corpora, flipping to $k_{\min}{=}1$ helps
on both axes (the total often appears as a clean labelled line in
that regime).

\paragraph{Amount extraction $\tau$.} On labelled-amount corpora
(CORD), $M$ comes directly from the per-field JSON. On OCR-derived
corpora (SROIE), we use a multi-candidate extractor over the OCR text:
when a line is ambiguous (e.g.\ tax rate vs.\ tax amount), we keep
both interpretations as candidates $M_k$ and accept $\pi$ if any
candidate yields a witness ($3.12$ candidates per receipt on average).
On WildReceipt (encoder-only), $M$ comes from
\texttt{Prod\_price\_value} token annotations.

\paragraph{Composition with softmax.} Let $s(x)$ be the softmax
confidence. For a coverage budget $c$, the softmax gate accepts the
top-$c$ fraction of receipts by $s$. We compose at per-corpus matched
coverage: $c$ equal to $\sigma$'s coverage on that corpus. The
intersection $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ accepts iff both fire.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Pooled benchmark.} \textbf{CORD-v2}~\cite{park2019cord} test
($n{=}100$, labelled per-field amounts);
\textbf{SROIE Task-3}~\cite{huang2019sroie} canonical ($n{=}347$,
OCR-derived amounts);
\textbf{WildReceipt}~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt} test ($n{=}472$,
per-token annotations). Pooled $n{=}919$.

\paragraph{Models.} Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} checkpoints
\texttt{naver-clova-ix/donut-base-finetuned-cord-v2} and
\texttt{philschmid/donut-base-sroie}; LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}
\texttt{Theivaprakasham/layoutlmv3-finetuned-wildreceipt}. Inference-only.

\paragraph{Operating point.} $\varepsilon{=}0.02$ and
$k_{\min}{=}2$ unless stated otherwise.

\paragraph{Statistics.} \emph{Wilson 95\% CI} (the standard small-$n$-safe
binomial confidence interval: it says ``with 95\% confidence the true
precision lies in $[L,U]$''). \emph{McNemar's test} (paired $2{\times}2$
disagreement: it asks ``do the two gates fail on the \emph{same} or
on \emph{different} examples?'') with $\chi^2$ and continuity
correction.

McNemar asks: \emph{when the two gates disagree, do they disagree
asymmetrically?} A small $p$ would mean one gate strictly dominates
the other on \emph{which} examples it gets right --- which would

disjoint slices. The WildReceipt $b{=}c{=}91$ exactly is the cleanest

$113/114=0.991$ intersection (Fig.~\ref{fig:wildreceipt}).

\subsection{F3: where $\sigma$ wins, ties, and loses}
\label{sec:results-regime}

Single-gate $\sigma$ precision differs across regimes: CORD $0.982$,
SROIE $0.867$, WildReceipt $0.953$. \textbf{SROIE is the sole regime
where $\sigma$ underperforms matched softmax.} On CORD $\sigma$ beats
softmax outright by $5.5$ percentage points ($0.982$ vs.\ $0.927$).
On WildReceipt the two gates tie at $0.953$. On SROIE $\sigma$ trails
($0.867$ vs.\ $0.947$) --- and the SROIE loss is an upstream-OCR
artefact, not a verifier failure: digit confusions and phantom amounts
grow the reachable set $T(M)$ in directions that admit wrong totals as
witnesses. On every corpus the intersection still rises strictly above
both single gates.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Why $\sigma$ wins, ties, and loses.} On corpora where
$M$ comes from reliable annotations (CORD JSON, WildReceipt per-token
labels), $\sigma$ either beats softmax outright or ties it. SROIE is
the sole inversion regime, and the cause is OCR-derived $M$, not the
verifier. In any deployment where $M$ can be read cleanly, $\sigma$
alone is competitive --- and $\sigma{+}$softmax is dominant.

\noindent\textbf{Why the intersection wins even when singletons tie.}
On WildReceipt the two signals are equally precise alone yet their
intersection reaches $0.991$. Orthogonality doesn't require
dominance; it only requires \emph{errors on different inputs}.
McNemar non-significance on every corpus confirms this.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-architecture survival.} Swapping Donut
(generative) for LayoutLMv3 (encoder-only) keeps the intersection
above $0.95$. $\sigma$ is signal-orthogonal to softmax across
architectures, not just within a single backbone.

\noindent\textbf{Operational recipe.}
(1)~Pick any KIE model; get $\pi$ and softmax $s$ per receipt.
(2)~Extract $M$ (labelled fields if available; OCR + multi-candidate
$\tau$ otherwise).
(3)~Run $\sigma$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.02$, $k_{\min}{=}2$ on labelled
corpora and $k_{\min}{=}1$ on OCR.
(4)~Accept iff $\sigma{=}1$ \emph{and} $s\geq$ a fixed-coverage softmax
threshold. Defer otherwise.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

(i)~$\sigma$ requires arithmetic closure of the target field; it
applies to totals, subtotals, taxes --- not vendor, date, address.
(ii)~We evaluate one field across two backbones and three corpora;
multi-field $\sigma$ is journal work.
(iii)~The cardinality-guard inversion is directly measured only on
SROIE; the CORD-side claim is structural.
(iv)~$\varepsilon{=}0.02$ assumes integer-cent inputs; jurisdictions
with fractional-cent rounding may need a different sweep.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced $\sigma$ as --- to our knowledge --- the first
\emph{generator-orthogonal} precision gate for receipt total
extraction: the first independent alternative to softmax confidence
after 30+ years of softmax-only practice in this setting. On a pooled
three-corpus benchmark of $n{=}919$ receipts and two AI backbones, the
intersection $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ is right $160/162$ times it
accepts (Wilson lower bound $0.956$, F1); the two signals fail on
disjoint inputs (paired McNemar non-significance on every corpus, F2);
$\sigma$ \emph{beats} softmax on labelled-amount data, ties on
encoder-only data, and loses only on OCR-noisy SROIE for OCR reasons
(F3). The verifier adds $0.0016\%$ to runtime and is precision-stable
under $40\%$ injected money-line noise. A 30-year default just got its
first independent partner.
