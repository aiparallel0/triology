\begin{abstract}
\boldmath
\linespread{1}\selectfont
Document-AI systems often extract itemized fields from receipts, with the ``total'' field being the most critical target. Errors in this field can cause major problems in downstream processes like tax filings, expense reports, and financial audits. The softmax layers in Document-AI models often produce miscalibrated predictions~\cite{guo2017calibration} and fail to identify these errors in isolation. To address this, we explore the \emph{sigma-verifier}: a validation layer operating outside the model that checks whether any subset of itemized lines on a receipt sums to the predicted total. We frame this as a 0/1-knapsack problem using the amounts printed on the receipt. We assessed the sigma-verifier on receipt-parsing systems based on two leading architectures, Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} and LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}, across three public benchmarks. The arithmetic cross-checking we employed is a well-established industrial practice, and we claim no novelty in the check itself. What we present is a measurement of this check as a signal independent of the model's own confidence. The key finding is a fourfold reduction in silent wrong totals. When combining the sigma-verifier with softmax across three corpora, we lower the silent-wrong-total rate among accepted receipts from 4.7\% to 1.1\%, achieving a 77\% relative reduction (55--81\% per corpus). The trade-off is coverage: acceptance drops from 38\% to 18\% of receipts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\emph{Receipt key-information extraction} (KIE) is the task of reading a
receipt image and returning named fields: vendor, date, taxes, and above all
the \emph{total}. Those outputs flow straight into tax filings, expense
pipelines, audit logs, and bookkeeping, and a silently wrong total is not
recoverable downstream. Commercial vendors (Expensify, QuickBooks, SAP
Concur) process millions of receipts per day, so an undetected wrong total at
that scale is a systematic failure, not an isolated one. No extractor
is accurate enough to remove that risk, which is why the standard mitigation
is not a better extractor but \emph{selective
prediction}~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,elyaniv2010foundations}: letting
the model decline to answer, saying ``I'm not sure,'' on inputs it cannot
reliably handle. That substitutes one problem for another. Every deployment
now needs a \emph{signal} on which to base the accept-or-defer call, and
which signal that should be is the question this paper takes up.

\noindent\textbf{The longstanding default and its flaw.} The default signal is
softmax confidence, the model's own estimate of how sure it is, computed as the geometric mean of the
probabilities it assigned to the tokens it actually
emitted~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph}.
Its flaw is structural rather than a matter of calibration: softmax is a
property of the \emph{generator itself}, so a model that hallucinates a
plausible total typically does so with high confidence, and the one signal
available to catch the error is produced by the very process that made it.
What a deployment wants instead is a second opinion computed from something
other than the model.

\noindent We propose $\sigma$ as that second
opinion\footnote{Throughout, $\sigma$ names the verifier
defined below, not the standard-deviation symbol.}.
Fig.~\ref{fig:scope} sets out the whole argument: an AI predicts a total,
standard practice trusts only the model's own softmax confidence, and
$\sigma$ checks the same number against the receipt's arithmetic, so the two
can be required to agree. $\sigma$ accepts the predicted total when some
subset of the monetary amounts visible on the receipt sums to it within
tolerance (Section~\ref{sec:method}). Such a witness exists by construction
on a well-formed receipt, because the total \emph{is} a sum of visible
amounts. The verifier is a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic program (DP),
with measured sub-millisecond $p_{99}$ latency
(Section~\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\noindent Silent failures are the expensive part of the problem: a
plausible wrong total is filed unchallenged and propagates downstream for
weeks before audit. Deferring therefore has to
be selective, because human
review is orders of magnitude costlier than an inference pass
(Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}). Regulation (EU AI Act,
financial-services model-risk rules) increasingly mandates documented
per-prediction trust signals, and $\sigma$ is computed on the document rather
than the model, so it produces a separate audit trail.

\noindent The principle generalizes beyond receipts. \emph{When a task's output
meets a verifiable structural rule, a deterministic verifier acts as a
confidence signal, statistically orthogonal to softmax because its errors arise
from document structure, not decoder logits.} The construction applies wherever a structural invariant is
checkable: balancing tables, schemas with referential constraints, code with
type checks, and logic with a solver.

We contribute the following. (i)~A measurement of $\sigma$ as a
selective-prediction signal on receipts (Section~\ref{sec:method}). The check
itself is established industrial practice and we claim no methodological
novelty for it; the contribution is the measurement, with the paired
statistics such a claim requires (Section~\ref{sec:related}). (ii)~Empirical validation on a pooled
three-corpus benchmark of $n{=}1019$ receipts spanning two backbones
(Donut generative, LayoutLMv3 encoder-only) and three regimes (CORD
labeled, SROIE OCR, WildReceipt encoder-only) with Wilson 95\%
precision lower bounds for every cell (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}).
(iii)~Statistical orthogonality via paired McNemar tests
(Section~\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}); a regime-mechanistic explanation of
where each signal wins (Section~\ref{sec:results-regime}); and robustness to
$40\%$ injected money-line noise plus a cardinality-guard ablation
(Section~\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent Signals that read the model have a long
history~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,elyaniv2010foundations,jaeger2024augrc}.
For sequence-to-sequence generators the standard signals come from the
generator's own token-level
probabilities~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph}
or from sample-based semantic
divergence~\cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024semantic}; Monte-Carlo dropout
and deep ensembles belong here as well. These differ in how they aggregate,
but every one of them interrogates the model. $\sigma$ is not a further
softmax aggregator; it is a signal that does not read the model at all.

\noindent Signals that read the output are older still: arithmetic
cross-checks of receipt totals are common engineering practice, and expense-management
software has used them for at least a decade. Program- and logic-level
verification of generative outputs appears in narrower settings such as
code-execution validation and faithful chain-of-thought, and structure-aware
verification appears for table reconstruction~\cite{nassar2022tableformer}.
$\sigma$ instantiates that spirit for receipt KIE, where the target admits
closed-form arithmetic over visible quantities. \emph{This is the gap we
address}: across this literature we could not locate such a check evaluated as
a softmax-orthogonal selective predictor on fixed off-the-shelf models, with
paired statistics across architectures. The check is old; the measurement is
what is missing.

\noindent The two backbones we verify are structurally opposite, since
$\sigma$ is model-agnostic and should hold across both.
Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} is \emph{generative} and OCR-free: a
vision-encoder-decoder that reads pixels and emits structured JSON one token
at a time. LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} is \emph{encoder-only}: it
consumes OCR text together with its layout coordinates and the page image, and
labels each token with a field. Their confidence scores are therefore
different objects (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), which is exactly why a verifier
that ignores both is worth measuring. Conformal
prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle} is complementary rather than
competing: it calibrates coverage from past prediction-truth pairs, whereas
$\sigma$ verifies one prediction against the document's own arithmetic.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Problem and notation.} Given a receipt image $x$, the KIE model emits a predicted
total, written $\pi(x)$, a non-negative rational number of currency
units; write $y(x)$ for the gold total. A \emph{selective predictor} is a rule
$g$ that makes one of two calls on every receipt: \emph{accept} the predicted
total, or \emph{reject} it and defer to a person. Two numbers describe such a
rule. Its \emph{coverage} $C(g)=\Pr[g{=}\text{accept}]$ is how often it
answers rather than defers, and its \emph{precision}
$P(g)=\Pr(\pi{=}y\mid g{=}\text{accept})$ is how often it is right when it
does answer. Raising one normally lowers the other, and this paper is about
buying precision at an acceptable price in coverage.

\noindent\textbf{The $\sigma$ decision rule.} Let $M(x)=\{m_1,\dots,m_n\}$ be the multiset of monetary amounts recovered from $x$ by an extractor $\tau$
(defined per corpus below). Fix a tolerance $\varepsilon\geq0$ in currency
units and a minimum witness size $k_{\min}\in\{1,2\}$, the smallest subset we
accept as evidence; without it a single line item that happens to equal the
total would always pass. Write the \emph{reachable set}, every total those
amounts can produce, as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
T(M)=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}m_i\,\Big|\,
   S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\,|S|\geq k_{\min}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
The rule then runs in three steps, shown here on a receipt printing
$\{12.50,\,8.00,\,4.20,\,3.20\}$ for which the model predicts $\pi{=}24.70$.

% Keeps the three-step procedure in one column; with the natural break
% step 1 was stranded alone at the foot of the previous column.
\Needspace*{8\baselineskip}
\begin{steps}
\item \textbf{Collect.} Extract every monetary amount printed on the receipt
into $M(x)$; here $n{=}4$.
\item \textbf{Reach.} Compute $T(M)$ by~(\ref{eq:reach}) with a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic
program (DP) over amounts held as integer cents, which avoids floating-point
surprises. Measured on CPU this step has median \latMedian~\latUnit{} and
$p_{99}$ of \latPNineNine~\latUnit{} per receipt (Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}).
\item \textbf{Compare.} Accept $\pi$ iff
$\inf_{t\in T(M)}|t-\pi|\leq\varepsilon$, that is, iff some subset sums to the
prediction within $\varepsilon$; otherwise defer. Here
$12.50{+}8.00{+}4.20=24.70$ is a witness, so $\sigma$ accepts. Had the model
predicted $24.90$, the nearest reachable total would be $24.70$ and $\sigma$
would defer.
\end{steps}

\noindent\textbf{Cardinality guard.} The correct $k_{\min}$ hinges on whether the total appears on the receipt as a labeled line. When amounts are labeled, that line is included in $M$, making a singleton witness $\pi{=}m_j$ a coincidence rather than the intended structural match. Thus, $k_{\min}{=}2$ excludes it. In OCR-derived corpora, the labeled total is often surfaced by OCR, rendering the singleton legitimate. Here, $k_{\min}{=}1$ is beneficial on both axes (Table~\ref{tab:guard}). We apply $k_{\min}{=}2$ on CORD and WildReceipt, while $k_{\min}{=}1$ on SROIE, selected \emph{without labels}: $k_{\min}{=}1$ if the extractor identifies a single amount equal to $\pi$ on more than a fraction $\theta$ of receipts, which is a computable property of the $(\pi,M)$ stream alone. The corpora separate cleanly ($0.07/0.05/0.41$), indicating the rule is insensitive to $\theta$ over $[0.1,0.4]$. This is a deployment-time rule, not a hyperparameter adjusted on labels.

\noindent\textbf{Amount extraction.} The extractor $\tau$ is the sole part of $\sigma$ that is corpus-specific. It is the method that generates $M$ from $x$. In labeled-amount corpora (CORD), $M$ is derived directly from the per-field JSON. In OCR-derived corpora (SROIE), $\tau$ functions as a multi-candidate extractor over the OCR text: when a line is ambiguous (e.g., tax rate vs. tax amount), it retains both interpretations as candidates $M_k$ and accepts $\pi$ if any candidate produces a witness (averaging $3.12$ candidates per receipt). On WildReceipt (encoder-only), $M$ is obtained from the per-token Prod_price_value annotations.

\noindent\textbf{Composition with softmax.} We compose the two rules at matched coverage. Let $s(x)$ denote the softmax confidence. For a coverage budget $c$, the softmax decision rule accepts the top-$c$ fraction of receipts based on $s$. We compose at per-corpus matched coverage, setting $c$ equal to $\sigma$'s coverage on that corpus, ensuring the two rules are compared at the same operating point rather than at the same threshold. Their combination $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ accepts if and only if both accept.
\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Datasets.} We use three public receipt benchmarks, chosen to span the labeling regimes that matter in practice.
CORD-v2~\cite{park2019cord} test+validation ($n{=}200$,
labeled per-field amounts; CORD-v2 \emph{train} is the Donut
fine-tuning split and is excluded to keep the evaluation
leakage-free); SROIE Task-3~\cite{huang2019sroie} canonical
($n{=}347$, OCR-derived amounts); WildReceipt
\cite{sun2021wildreceipt} test ($n{=}472$, per-token annotations).
Pooled $n{=}\poolN{}$.

\noindent\textbf{Models.} Both backbones are off-the-shelf and inference-only: Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} fine-tuned on CORD-v2 and on SROIE, and
LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} fine-tuned on WildReceipt.

\noindent\textbf{Settings.} We set $\varepsilon{=}0.02$ throughout, and $k_{\min}$ follows the label-free cardinality-guard rule of
Section~\ref{sec:method} ($k_{\min}{=}2$ on CORD and WildReceipt,
$k_{\min}{=}1$ on SROIE). The fixed-$k_{\min}$ sweep of
Table~\ref{tab:guard} is the only departure, and is explicitly an
ablation of that rule.

\noindent\textbf{Statistics.} Per-cell precision carries Wilson 95\% confidence
intervals~\cite{wilson1927ci}, and agreement
between the two decision rules is tested with paired
McNemar~\cite{mcnemar1947note} ($\chi^2$ with continuity correction).

\noindent\textbf{Softmax across architectures.} Softmax means a different object on each architecture. For Donut
the softmax score is the geometric mean of per-token probabilities
over the predicted total_value span in the decoder's
autoregressive output; for LayoutLMv3, the geometric mean of the
predicted-class probability on input tokens tagged Total_value. Both are the
model's self-assessed probability of its emitted total-value tokens, so we
treat them as the architecture-appropriate ``model self-confidence'' but not
as numerically interchangeable.

\noindent\textbf{Extractor per experiment.} Two extractor settings appear in this paper. The multi-candidate
extractor $\tau$ of Section~\ref{sec:method} is the default and produces every
headline result; a fixed single-candidate extractor is used only in the two
ablations (Tables~\ref{tab:guard} and~\ref{tab:robust}), where holding $\tau$
fixed isolates the variable being tested. This is why the $r{=}0$ row of
Table~\ref{tab:robust} shows a different CORD coverage from the headline: a
different extractor, not a conflicting result.

\noindent Table~\ref{tab:headline-pooled} reports pooled results across all
$1{,}019$ receipts. The combination of $\sigma$ and softmax is the only
configuration that clears the $0.95$ precision floor required for fully
automated processing, achieving a Wilson 95\% lower bound
of $0.961$; neither rule alone reaches it ($0.918$ and $0.927$).

\noindent The gain costs coverage: the combination accepts only $18.1\%$ of
receipts against $37.9\%$ for either rule alone: roughly four receipts in five
still go to a person, so this is triage rather than automation. Whether that
trade is worthwhile is a deployment question. With human review costing $\rho$
inference passes ($\rho\in[10^2,10^3]$) and a silent wrong total costing
$\kappa$, moving from the softmax-matched row of Table~\ref{tab:headline-pooled}
to the combination pays whenever $\kappa\gtrsim13\rho$: a condition, not a
universal claim. It holds in high-stakes financial pipelines and not in
low-stakes bulk capture, where matched-coverage softmax is the better choice.

rather than the pool is the headline; Fig.~\ref{fig:wildreceipt} singles out
the WildReceipt case.

\subsection{Do the Two Signals Fail on the Same Receipts?}
\label{sec:results-mcnemar}

\begin{table}[t]

\textbf{Pooled}            & 185 & 188 & 0.011 & 0.917 \\

\noindent Table~\ref{tab:mcnemar} answers this directly. If the two failed together, combining them would offer
little benefit: you would simply be applying two filters that agree with each
other most of the time.

\noindent The test counts two kinds of disagreement.
$b$ counts receipts where $\sigma$ is correct but softmax is not,
mistakes that $\sigma$ catches but softmax misses. $c$ counts the
reverse, receipts where softmax is correct but $\sigma$ is not. If
$b$ and $c$ are roughly equal, neither signal is consistently better
than the other; they are catching different mistakes.

\noindent The counts are balanced. On WildReceipt, $b$ and $c$ are
exactly equal at $91$: $\sigma$ and softmax each rescue an identical number of
receipts the other misses, and every $p$-value exceeds $0.6$, so there is no
significant asymmetry in either direction. This is the key evidence that the
two signals are independent: they fail on different receipts, so requiring
both to agree filters out errors neither could catch alone. Strictly,
McNemar non-rejection is absence of evidence for asymmetry rather
than positive evidence of error-decorrelation; the direct
correlation-on-errors test (Section~\ref{sec:results-control})
supplies the latter.

\subsection{Is the Gain Genuine or Just Coverage Shrink?}
\label{sec:results-control}

\noindent The obvious objection is that combining any two
better-than-random decision rules with imperfectly correlated errors raises
precision and lowers coverage, so the headline may be nothing more than that
generic effect. We re-analyze the same per-receipt accept/correct vectors, with
no new inference.
Define the lift $L=P(\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax})-P(\text{softmax at matched
coverage})$ and compare it at identical coverage against two controls: (A)~a
\emph{random} decision rule accepting a uniform random subset of size
$|\sigma{=}1|$, which carries no information and gives pure coverage-shrink,
and (B)~a \emph{correlated} decision rule
$\text{softmax}'=\text{softmax}+\text{noise}$, calibrated so
$\text{softmax}'$ matches $\sigma$'s standalone precision while being built
from softmax itself. A paired bootstrap ($B{=}4000$) recomputes $L_\sigma$ and
both control lifts on the \emph{same} resample and takes the difference.

\noindent Pooled, $\sigma$'s lift
exceeds the random decision rule by $\orthDrand$ (95\,\% CI
$[\orthDrandLo,\orthDrandHi]$, one-sided $p{=}\orthDrandP$) and the correlated
one by $\orthDcorr$ ($[\orthDcorrLo,\orthDcorrHi]$, $p{=}\orthDcorrP$). We
also measure error decorrelation directly inside the softmax-accepted set,
asking whether $\sigma$ rejects precisely the softmax-wrong receipts: the phi
coefficient between the two rules' errors is $\phi{=}\orthPhi$ (permutation
$p{=}\orthPhiP$). That is positive evidence of
orthogonality with no coverage-shrink confound, which the paired-McNemar result
of Section~\ref{sec:results-mcnemar} only implies indirectly.

\noindent The orthogonality is supported, but we claim it narrowly: the pooled
effect is \emph{small} ($\orthDrand$/$\orthDcorr$ precision lift) and does
\emph{not} hold corpus by corpus, so the claim is pooled and statistically
significant, not per-corpus and not large.

\smallskip
\label{sec:results-percorpus-rigor}
\noindent Per-corpus, the picture is weaker. Exact small-sample analysis of
the leakage-free combination cells (CORD $54/55$, SROIE $15/15$,
WildReceipt $113/114$). Lower bounds agree across estimators
(Clopper--Pearson, Jeffreys, Wilson): CORD $\rgCordWil$, SROIE
$\rgSroieWil$, WildReceipt $\rgWrWil$; receipt-weighted pool
$182/184$ (Wilson LB $\rgPoolWil$), leave-one-corpus-out worst
$\rgLocoMinWil$. A one-sided exact-binomial TOST (H$_0$:
$p\le0.95$) clears the floor \emph{individually only on WildReceipt}
($p=\rgTostWrP$); CORD ($p=\rgTostCordP$) and SROIE
($p=\rgTostSroieP$) do \emph{not} pass (consistent with $>0.95$ but
underpowered), while the pool does ($p=\rgTostPoolP$). A
within-corpus permutation test of $\sigma$'s orthogonal contribution
gives combined Stouffer $p=\rgAFiveStouffer$ and Fisher
$p=\rgAFiveFisher$: decorrelation holds within every corpus, not
only pooled.

\subsection{Does Sigma Lower Risk at Matched Coverage?}
\label{sec:results-riskcov}

\noindent Statistical orthogonality is necessary but not sufficient for
deployment value, which turns on whether the $\sigma$ decision rule beats simply
thresholding softmax. We compare risk--coverage at matched coverage, integrated
to an AURC~\cite{geifman2019aurc} (lower better). Pooled, AURC is
$\rcAURCsoft$ for softmax-alone against $\rcAURCsig$ for the $\sigma$ rule, a
paired-bootstrap difference of $\rcAURCd$ (95\,\% CI
$[\rcAURCdLo,\rcAURCdHi]$, one-sided $p{=}\rcAURCp$); the interval excludes
zero, so the $\sigma$ rule lowers AURC at matched coverage. We do not
over-read it: the absolute difference is
small and the lift costs coverage, so the operational gain is modest.

\subsection{When Does Each Signal Win, and Why?}
\label{sec:results-regime}

\noindent Each signal owns a regime. On CORD (clean per-field labels) $\sigma$
beats softmax by $3.0$ points ($0.990$ against $0.960$ at matched coverage);
on WildReceipt the two tie at $0.953$; on SROIE (OCR-derived $M$) $\sigma$
trails ($0.863$ against $0.945$). We attribute the SROIE loss to upstream OCR
rather than to the verifier: digit confusions and phantom amounts inflate
$T(M)$ with coincidental witnesses. The data support this. Of the $73$ receipts
$\sigma$ accepts on SROIE, the $10$ it accepts wrongly carry more extracted
amounts than the $63$ it accepts correctly ($6.7$ against $4.3$; one-sided
permutation test, $B{=}20{,}000$, $p{=}0.004$), while candidate readings per
line do not differ ($3.30$ against $3.33$, $p{=}0.57$). What matters is how
many amounts OCR puts on the page, not how many ways each line is read. With
$10$ false accepts this supports the account rather than settling it; a
hand-corrected subsample test is future work.

\noindent The check degrades gracefully under corruption. We corrupt the data deliberately,
injecting from $0\%$ to $40\%$ false line-item amounts over $10$ seeds.
Table~\ref{tab:robust} shows coverage falling steadily ($26.7\%$ to $9.1\%$)

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Where $\sigma$ works.} Combining helps because orthogonality needs only errors on
different inputs, not dominance. The gain survives swapping Donut for
LayoutLMv3, so $\sigma$ is signal-orthogonal to softmax across architectures.
Six limits bound that claim.

\noindent\textbf{What $\sigma$ cannot do.} (i)~$\sigma$ requires
arithmetic closure of the target field; totals, subtotals, and taxes,
not vendor/date/address. (ii)~$\varepsilon{=}0.02$ assumes
integer-cent inputs; fractional-cent rounding jurisdictions may need
a different sweep. (iii)~$\sigma$ assumes $\pi$ is a parsed numeric
scalar; format-hallucinated outputs require an upstream normalizer we
do not benchmark. (iv)~In the OCR-derived regime $\sigma$ is valuable
only in combination, not as a single decision rule
(Section~\ref{sec:results-regime}). (v)~We compare against softmax only; the
model-internal alternatives of Section~\ref{sec:related} all inherit the
failure mode $\sigma$ exists to catch, and we know of no published
document-external verifier to compare against. (vi)~The headline is a
per-corpus claim: the SROIE combined-rule cell ($n{=}15$) supports only a
consistency statement, and the cardinality-guard inversion is measured
directly only on SROIE. We evaluate one field across two backbones and three
corpora; multi-field $\sigma$ is journal work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented the sigma-verifier, a model-agnostic validation
layer that checks whether any subset of extracted itemized lines sums to the
predicted total, and evaluated it across two document AI architectures and
three receipt datasets. Combining it with the model's softmax confidence
reduces the silent-wrong-total rate among accepted receipts from 4.7\% to
1.1\% pooled, an $\approx$77\% relative reduction and roughly four-fold fewer
audit-exposing failures, at the coverage cost set out above; the pooled lift
is individually significant only on WildReceipt. The broader point is that the
signal cost nothing to compute and came from the document rather than the
model, so wherever a model's output must satisfy a checkable structural
invariant, the same construction is available.
