\title{Sigma: A Structure-Verified, Softmax-Orthogonal\\
Precision Gate for Receipt Total Extraction}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Submission}
\IEEEauthorblockA{ASYU 2026}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Selective prediction for document key-information extraction (KIE) commonly
relies on softmax confidence, which is by construction a property of the
generator and can remain high on confidently wrong outputs. We introduce
$\sigma$, a precision gate that is \emph{orthogonal} to the generator: given
a predicted total $\pi$ and the set $M$ of monetary amounts extracted from a
receipt, $\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff there exists a subset $S \subseteq M$ with
$|S| \ge k_{\min}$ whose sum equals $\pi$ within tolerance $\varepsilon$.
The verifier is a $0/1$-knapsack dynamic program with $p_{99}$ latency below
$1\,\text{ms}$ per receipt. On CORD-v2, $\sigma$ achieves precision $0.982$
($54/55$) at $55\%$ coverage versus $0.927$ for softmax at matched coverage,
and the $22$ receipts that $\sigma$ accepts but softmax rejects are
\emph{all correct} (Wilson $95\%$ CI $[0.851, 1.000]$). On SROIE,
$\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ attains precision $1.000$ ($15/15$, Wilson
$[0.796, 1.000]$). McNemar tests on both corpora are non-significant on
accept-correctness counts (CORD $p=0.755$, SROIE $p=0.627$), supporting the
central claim: $\sigma$ and softmax are not redundant signals --- they fire
on \emph{different receipts}, so their intersection achieves near-perfect
precision. We characterise the cross-regime behaviour (labeled-amount CORD
vs.\ OCR-derived SROIE), trace the regime gap to $\tau$-extraction quality
via a per-receipt failure taxonomy, and report a cardinality-guard ablation
that exposes a regime-dependent inversion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Document KIE models such as DONUT~\cite{kim2022donut} and
LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} are increasingly deployed in
financial pipelines (expense management, accounting, audit) where
\emph{precision on the trusted slice} matters more than aggregate accuracy.
A downstream system that automatically books a receipt to a ledger cannot
afford a $5\%$ error rate on accepted predictions; it would rather defer
$80\%$ to humans and trust the $20\%$ it accepts.

The standard selective-prediction signal for such deferral is
softmax-derived confidence (e.g., the geometric mean of per-token
probabilities)~\cite{geifman2017selective}. But softmax is a property of
the \emph{generator}: when the generator hallucinates a plausible total,
softmax is often still confident. A precision signal that is independent
of generator logits --- and that therefore makes mistakes on
\emph{different examples} --- could be intersected with softmax to push
precision toward $1.0$ on the surviving slice.

We propose $\sigma$, a verifier-based precision gate that reads the
arithmetic structure of the receipt. Let $M = \{m_1, \dots, m_n\}$ be the
amounts extracted from the receipt's money lines (line-item prices, taxes,
tips) and $\pi$ the model's predicted total. $\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff
there is a witness subset $S \subseteq M$ with $|S| \ge k_{\min}$ and
$|\sum S - \pi| \le \varepsilon$. The witness exists because well-formed
receipts are \emph{closed} under arithmetic: the total \emph{is} a sum of
visible amounts. When $\sigma$ accepts, the verifier has reconstructed
the model's answer from the receipt's own contents; when it rejects,
the model has produced a number that no subset of the receipt's
amounts can explain.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A precision-orthogonal selective predictor $\sigma$ for receipt
    total extraction, formalised as a cardinality-guarded subset-sum
    verifier with sub-millisecond $p_{99}$ latency.
    \item Empirical validation across two corpora and two regimes (CORD-v2,
    labeled amounts; SROIE, OCR-derived amounts) on a DONUT backbone:
    $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ achieves precision $1.000$ on both corpora
    and $\sigma$-only precision of $1.000$ on CORD.
    \item McNemar-based evidence that $\sigma$ and softmax accept
    \emph{different} correct receipts at near-equal rates, so the
    contribution is orthogonality, not strict dominance.
    \item A regime analysis explaining why $\sigma$'s headline precision
    drops on OCR-derived corpora, located mechanistically in $\tau$-extraction
    quality rather than in the verifier itself.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
Selective classification has a long history~\cite{geifman2017selective,
elyaniv2010foundations}; sequence-level extensions typically rely on
generator-internal signals (per-token entropy, sequence
probability)~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty}. For document KIE specifically,
recent work has focused on the generator side: improving the backbone
(DONUT~\cite{kim2022donut}, LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}) or the
training objective. Structure-based verification --- using the document's
\emph{own} content to check the model's output --- has been explored for
table extraction~\cite{nassar2022tableformer} and arithmetic-bearing
documents in narrow forms, but to our knowledge has not been formalised
as a precision-orthogonal selective predictor on top of an existing KIE
model.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Problem}
Given a receipt image $x$, a KIE model produces a predicted total
$\pi(x) \in \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}$ along with a generation trace from which a
softmax confidence $s(x) \in [0, 1]$ can be derived. Let $y(x)$ be the
gold total. A selective predictor is a binary gate
$g: x \mapsto \{\text{accept}, \text{reject}\}$ inducing coverage
$C(g) = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{1}_{g(x)=\text{accept}}]$ and precision
$P(g) = \Pr(\pi(x)=y(x) \mid g(x)=\text{accept})$. We seek gates with
$P(g)$ close to $1$ at non-trivial $C(g)$.

\subsection{The $\sigma$ Gate}
Let $M(x) = \{m_1, \dots, m_n\}$ be the multiset of monetary amounts
extracted from $x$ (Section~\ref{sec:tau}). Fix a cardinality threshold
$k_{\min} \in \{1, 2\}$ and a tolerance $\varepsilon \ge 0$. Define the
\emph{reachable set}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
T(M) = \left\{ \sum_{i \in S} m_i \,\Big|\, S \subseteq \{1,\dots,n\},\ |S| \ge k_{\min} \right\}.
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff $\inf_{t \in T(M)} |t - \pi| \le \varepsilon$.
$T(M)$ is computed by a standard $0/1$-knapsack dynamic program with
state $(i, s)$ tracking ``is sum $s$ reachable using a subset of the
first $i$ items.'' Values are stored as integer cents to avoid
floating-point comparisons.

\textbf{Cardinality guard.} The choice $k_{\min} = 2$ excludes singleton
witnesses. Without it, any receipt where a line item happens to equal the
total ($\pi = m_j$ for some $j$) is trivially accepted, and
$\sigma$-precision degrades toward the base rate. The guard's value is
regime-dependent, which we analyse empirically in
Section~\ref{sec:ablation}.

\subsection{$\tau$-Extraction}
\label{sec:tau}
$M(x)$ is extracted from the receipt by parsing money-bearing lines
(prices, taxes, tips, line items) and discarding lines that are not
amounts (registration numbers, phone numbers, dates, percent rates).
On labeled-amount corpora (CORD-v2) money lines are directly available
from the field annotations. On OCR-derived corpora (SROIE) we run a
$\tau$-extractor over the OCR text that combines pattern matching with
a multi-candidate strategy: multiple plausible interpretations of an
ambiguous line are kept and $\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ if any candidate
yields a witness. We refer to this as $\sigma$ over a union of reachable
sets $T = \bigcup_k T(M_k)$.

\subsection{Composition with Softmax}
Let $s(x)$ be a softmax-derived confidence (we use the geometric mean of
per-token probabilities of the generated total string). For a coverage
budget $c$, the softmax gate accepts the top-$c$ fraction by $s$.
Composing the two gates yields four signals of interest: $\sigma$ alone,
softmax alone, their intersection
$\sigma \sqcap \text{softmax}$, and their union
$\sigma \sqcup \text{softmax}$. Our central empirical claim is that
$\sigma$ and softmax accept different receipts at near-equal correctness
rates, so the intersection achieves higher precision than either alone.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Corpora.} We evaluate on two receipt KIE benchmarks:
\textbf{CORD-v2} (test split, $n{=}100$ receipts), with labeled per-field
amounts; and \textbf{SROIE Task-3} (canonical $n{=}347$ receipts) with
OCR-derived amounts (Task-1 OCR from \texttt{darentang/sroie}).

\textbf{Model.} We use publicly released DONUT
checkpoints~\cite{kim2022donut}:
\texttt{naver-clova-ix/donut-base-finetuned-cord-v2} for CORD and
\texttt{philschmid/donut-base-sroie} for SROIE. Both produce a
\texttt{total\_price}/\texttt{total} field via constrained generation.

\textbf{Operating point.} $\varepsilon = 0.02$ (currency-minor-unit cents)
and $k_{\min} = 2$ unless stated otherwise. A tolerance sweep
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) shows $\varepsilon$ is not load-bearing on
CORD.

\textbf{Statistics.} Wilson $95\%$ confidence intervals are reported for
all proportions; bootstrap CIs ($B = 2000$) are reported in the
significance section. McNemar tests use $\chi^2$ with continuity
correction; for small discordant cell counts we cross-check against the
exact binomial.

\subsection{Statistical Orthogonality}
\label{sec:mcnemar}
On CORD the McNemar paired test on accept-correctness yields
$b=22, c=19$, $\chi^2=0.098$, $p=0.755$; on SROIE $b=50, c=56$,
$\chi^2=0.236$, $p=0.627$. Neither is significant at $\alpha=0.05$.
This is the right outcome for our claim: the two gates accept
\emph{about the same number} of correct receipts, but \emph{different
receipts}. The orthogonality lives in the disagreement cells $b$ and
$c$, not in their imbalance.

\subsection{Cardinality Guard Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:kmin} reports the SROIE cardinality-guard ablation.
Removing the guard ($k_{\min}{=}1$) raises coverage from $0.029$ to
$0.161$ at marginal precision cost ($0.929$ vs.\ $0.900$): on the
OCR regime, many singleton-money receipts trivially admit the total
itself as a witness, so the unguarded $\sigma$ collapses toward base
rate. On labeled-amount corpora (CORD bucket analysis,
Table~\ref{tab:buckets}), the relationship inverts: the guard preserves
precision because labeled lines rarely contain a literal total.

\subsection{Latency}
The verifier's dynamic program runs in median $5\,\mu\text{s}$, $p_{95} =
74\,\mu\text{s}$, $p_{99} = 547\,\mu\text{s}$, $\max = 780\,\mu\text{s}$
per receipt over $n=347$ SROIE receipts. Compared to a single DONUT
inference (tens of milliseconds on a single GPU), $\sigma$'s overhead is
negligible.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{The Cross-Regime Split}
$\sigma$'s absolute precision differs between CORD ($0.982$) and SROIE
($0.867$). The mechanism is $\tau$-extraction quality, not the verifier.
On CORD, $M(x)$ comes from field annotations and is essentially clean;
on SROIE it comes from OCR, where digit confusions, segmentation errors,
and spurious amounts (registration numbers misread as money) introduce
distractors into $M$. A per-receipt failure taxonomy on CORD shows that
failures concentrate in bucket $1$ (single-money receipts where no
multi-element witness exists) and in $\tau$-too-negative cases (rounding
into the gold-disagree side of $\varepsilon$). On SROIE, failures shift
toward \emph{far-miss} and \emph{$\tau$-too-negative} categories driven by
OCR-introduced phantom amounts. This is consistent with our claim that
$\sigma$'s headline gap is a feature of the upstream OCR pipeline, not of
the verifier.

\subsection{Robustness}
Under synthetic money-line noise (digit swap, decimal shift, drop,
spurious-add) applied at rates $\{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4\}$ to CORD, over
$10$ seeds, $\sigma$'s precision remains in $[0.949, 0.980]$ with the
bootstrap $95\%$ CI containing $0.94$ even at $40\%$ corruption.
Coverage falls roughly $4\times$ from $0.27$ to $0.09$ over the same
range. $\sigma$ degrades \emph{gracefully on the precision axis} ---
the gate becomes selective, not wrong, under input corruption.

\subsection{Limitations}
$\sigma$ requires structured arithmetic in the document --- it would
not extend to fields without an in-document witness (vendor names, dates).
We evaluate one field (total) on one model family (DONUT) on two corpora;
cross-architecture validation on LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}
and a full softmax baseline on WildReceipt~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt}
require an OCR archive that is not bundled with the public dataset
release. We report $\sigma$-alone numbers on WildReceipt
(coverage $0.453$, precision $0.953$, $n{=}472$) but defer the matched
softmax baseline to an extended version.

\section{Conclusion}
$\sigma$ is a structure-verified, generator-orthogonal precision gate
for receipt total extraction. Across two corpora and two regimes,
$\sigma \sqcap \text{softmax}$ reaches precision $1.000$ at non-trivial
coverage; on CORD the receipts $\sigma$ accepts and softmax rejects are
\emph{all} correct, with a Wilson $95\%$ CI lower bound of $0.851$. The
orthogonality is statistical (McNemar $p > 0.6$ on both corpora on
accept-correctness counts) and mechanistic (the two gates use disjoint
evidence: generator logits vs.\ receipt arithmetic). $\sigma$'s verifier
costs under one millisecond per receipt at $p_{99}$. We expect the
strongest near-term gains in financial-document automation, where
deferral-on-rejection is operationally cheap and precision on the
accepted slice is the bottleneck.
