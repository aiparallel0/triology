\begin{abstract}
Document-AI systems extract named fields from receipts; the
\texttt{total} is the highest-value target and a silently wrong total
propagates downstream into tax filings, expense reports, and audits.
For 30+ years the only signal practitioners have had for deciding
when to trust a predicted total has been the model's own softmax
confidence, a single scalar that miscalibrates in a familiar
way~\cite{guo2017calibration}: a model can score $0.97$ on a receipt
and still be wrong. We propose $\sigma$, an arithmetic
verifier that ignores the model and asks of the receipt directly,
\emph{does any subset of the visible line amounts sum to the predicted
total?} The check is a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic program; it
runs in roughly $1.5\,\mu$s per receipt, well under $0.01\%$ of
end-to-end inference time on Donut. The witness exists by construction
on well-formed receipts; where it does not (rounding-only lines,
post-subtotal discounts, hidden service charges) $\sigma$ silently
rejects valid totals, and we localise its losses to such violations.
To our knowledge $\sigma$ is the first peer-reviewed formalisation of
arithmetic verification as a softmax-orthogonal selective-prediction
signal for receipt total extraction, with paired statistical analysis
across two architectures (Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} generative,
LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} encoder-only) and three corpora
(CORD-v2~\cite{park2019cord}, SROIE Task-3~\cite{huang2019sroie},
WildReceipt~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt}; pooled $n{=}919$).
The intersection $\sigma \sqcap \text{softmax}$ accepts $162$
receipts and is right on $160$ (Wilson 95\% lower bound $0.956$, above
the operational floor for hands-off shipping); each gate alone sits
below $0.92$. The precision lift costs coverage: the intersection
fires on $17.6\%$ of receipts versus $37.4\%$ for either single gate,
a trade that is appropriate when one silent wrong total costs more
than one extra human review. Paired McNemar tests are non-significant
on every corpus; the cleanest case is WildReceipt, where $\sigma$ and
softmax both reach $0.953$ alone but disagree exactly symmetrically
($b{=}c{=}91$), and the intersection rises to $0.991$ ($113/114$).
Where the receipt amount multiset is read from clean labels, $\sigma$
matches or beats softmax (CORD $0.982$ vs.\ $0.927$, WildReceipt tie);
on OCR-derived amounts (SROIE) it trails ($0.867$ vs.\ $0.947$),
which we attribute to upstream OCR digit confusions inflating the
reachable subset-sum set with phantom witnesses. Precision stays
within $[0.949, 0.980]$ under up to $40\%$ injected money-line noise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\emph{Receipt key-information extraction} (KIE) means: a model reads a
receipt image and outputs named fields: vendor, date, taxes, and
especially the \texttt{total}. The outputs flow directly into
tax filings, expense pipelines, audit logs, and
bookkeeping~\cite{rombach2026conformal}. A silently wrong
\texttt{total} is not recoverable downstream; commercial vendors
(Expensify, Concur, QuickBooks, SAP Concur) process millions of
receipts per day and a wrong total at scale translates into real audit
exposure. The standard mitigation is \emph{selective
prediction}~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,elyaniv2010foundations}: let
the model defer (say ``I'm not sure'') on inputs it cannot reliably
handle. Every deployment needs a signal to drive the defer-versus-accept
decision.

\noindent\textbf{The longstanding default and its flaw.} For the modern
era of neural KIE the only such signal in this setting has been
\emph{softmax confidence}: when a model produces an answer one token
at a time, softmax converts its internal scores into probabilities
that sum to $1$ at each token position. The geometric mean of the
probabilities the model assigned to the tokens it actually emitted is
the standard ``how sure am I''
number~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,vashurin2025benchmark,
fadeeva2023polygraph}. The flaw is structural: softmax is a property
of the \emph{generator itself}, so a model that hallucinates a
plausible total often does so confidently. Industrial OCR pipelines
have long applied ad-hoc arithmetic cross-checks; what has been
missing is a peer-reviewed formalisation of such a check as an
independent (model-internal-free) verification signal with paired
statistical analysis against softmax.

\noindent\textbf{Our contribution.} We propose
$\sigma$\footnote{Throughout, $\sigma$ names the verifier gate
defined below, not the standard-deviation symbol.} as that independent
signal (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigm}). Given the model's
predicted total $\pi$ and the multiset $M$ of monetary amounts visible
on the receipt (line items, taxes, tips, subtotals), $\sigma$ accepts
$\pi$ iff some subset $S\subseteq M$ with $|S|\geq k_{\min}$ sums to
$\pi$ within tolerance $\varepsilon$. The witness exists by
construction on well-formed receipts: the total \emph{is} a sum of
visible amounts. The verifier is a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic
program (DP), with sub-millisecond $p_{99}$ latency.

\noindent\textbf{Why this matters operationally.}
(i)~Silent failures are the expensive failure mode: a plausible-looking
wrong total is filed unchallenged and propagates for weeks before audit.
(ii)~Human-in-the-loop verification costs $10^2$--$10^3\times$ a single
inference pass; the whole point of automation is to defer to humans only
when needed.
(iii)~Regulation (EU AI Act, FDA SaMD-style continuous-monitoring
requirements, financial-services model-risk-management rules) increasingly
mandates documented per-prediction trust signals. $\sigma$ is computed on
the document, not the model, so it produces a separate audit trail.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~$\sigma$, to our knowledge the
first \emph{peer-reviewed formalisation of arithmetic verification as
a softmax-orthogonal selective-prediction signal} for receipt KIE
(\S\ref{sec:method}). (ii)~Empirical validation on a pooled
three-corpus benchmark of $n{=}919$ receipts spanning two backbones
(Donut generative, LayoutLMv3 encoder-only) and three regimes (CORD
labelled, SROIE OCR, WildReceipt encoder-only) with Wilson 95\%
precision lower bounds for every cell (\S\ref{sec:results-headline}).
(iii)~Statistical orthogonality via paired McNemar tests
(\S\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}); a regime-mechanistic explanation of
where each signal wins (\S\ref{sec:results-regime}); and robustness to
$40\%$ injected money-line noise plus a cardinality-guard ablation
(\S\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Selective prediction has a long history~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,
elyaniv2010foundations,jaeger2024augrc}; for sequence-to-sequence
generators (such as Donut) the standard signals are derived from the
generator's own token-level probabilities~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,
vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph} or from sample-based
semantic divergence~\cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024semantic}. We
use the geometric mean of per-token probabilities of the predicted
total field as a single softmax baseline. $\sigma$ is \emph{not} a
different softmax aggregator; it is a different \emph{signal} that
does not read the model at all.

\noindent\textbf{Industrial prior art and program-level verification.}
Arithmetic cross-checks of receipt totals are common engineering
practice in commercial document-processing pipelines (expense-management
software has applied them for at least a decade). The gap $\sigma$
fills is the peer-reviewed analysis of such a check as a
softmax-orthogonal selective-prediction signal with paired statistical
machinery and cross-architecture validation, not the act of checking
arithmetic itself. More broadly, program- and logic-level verification
of generative-model outputs has appeared in narrower settings
(code-execution validation, faithful chain-of-thought reasoning);
$\sigma$ inherits that spirit and instantiates it for receipt KIE,
where the verification target is closed-form arithmetic over visible
quantities.

We evaluate on two architectures. Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} is a
\emph{generative} vision-encoder-decoder backbone that produces
structured JSON. LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} is an
\emph{encoder-only} token-classification backbone (it tags each input
token with a class such as \texttt{Total\_value}). Their softmax
confidences are architecture-specific but conceptually the same:
average per-token probability on the predicted total-value tokens.

Structure-aware verification has appeared for table
reconstruction~\cite{nassar2022tableformer} and arithmetic forms in
narrow settings; what is new here is the formalisation of an
arithmetic check as a softmax-orthogonal selective predictor on top of
fixed, off-the-shelf KIE models, with paired statistical analysis of
the orthogonality claim across two architectures. Conformal
prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle,rombach2026conformal} gives
calibrated coverage but no verifier-style precision gate; the two are
complementary: conformal calibrates a confidence using past
prediction-versus-truth pairs, $\sigma$ verifies a single prediction
against the document's own arithmetic, and they can be stacked.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Problem.} Given a receipt image $x$, a KIE model emits a
predicted total $\pi(x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$. Let $y(x)$ be the
gold total. A selective predictor is a binary gate
$g:x\mapsto\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}$ with
\emph{coverage} $C(g)=\Pr[g{=}\text{accept}]$ (how often it doesn't
defer) and \emph{precision}
$P(g)=\Pr(\pi{=}y\mid g{=}\text{accept})$ (how often it's right when
it doesn't defer).

\noindent\textbf{The $\sigma$ gate.} Let $M(x)=\{m_1,\dots,m_n\}$ be the
multiset of monetary amounts extracted from $x$. Fix
$k_{\min}\in\{1,2\}$ (the smallest subset size we'll accept as a
witness; without this, a single line item that happens to equal the
total would always pass) and tolerance $\varepsilon\geq 0$ (in
currency units). Define the reachable set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
T(M)=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}m_i\,\Big|\,
   S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\,|S|\geq k_{\min}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff $\inf_{t\in T(M)}|t-\pi|\leq\varepsilon$:
some subset of receipt amounts sums to the predicted total within
$\varepsilon$. $T(M)$ is computed by a textbook $0/1$-knapsack
DP. Amounts are stored as integer cents to avoid floating-point
surprises. Empirically $p_{99}{=}0.55\,\text{ms}$ per receipt
(Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}).

\noindent\textbf{Cardinality guard.} The right $k_{\min}$ depends on
whether the total typically appears on the receipt as a labelled
line. On labelled-amount corpora the labelled line is part of $M$ and
a singleton witness $\pi{=}m_j$ is a coincidence rather than the
intended structural match, so we set $k_{\min}{=}2$ to exclude it; on
OCR-derived corpora the OCR step often surfaces the labelled total
itself, and the singleton witness is then legitimate, so $k_{\min}{=}1$
helps on both axes (Table~\ref{tab:guard}). The default in this paper
is $k_{\min}{=}2$ on CORD and WildReceipt, $k_{\min}{=}1$ on SROIE.

\noindent\textbf{Amount extraction $\tau$.} On labelled-amount corpora
(CORD), $M$ comes directly from the per-field JSON. On OCR-derived
corpora (SROIE), we use a multi-candidate extractor over the OCR text:
when a line is ambiguous (e.g.\ tax rate vs.\ tax amount), we keep
both interpretations as candidates $M_k$ and accept $\pi$ if any
candidate yields a witness ($3.12$ candidates per receipt on average).
On WildReceipt (encoder-only), $M$ comes from
\texttt{Prod\_price\_value} token annotations.

\noindent\textbf{Composition with softmax.} Let $s(x)$ be the softmax
confidence. For a coverage budget $c$, the softmax gate accepts the
top-$c$ fraction of receipts by $s$. We compose at per-corpus matched
coverage: $c$ equal to $\sigma$'s coverage on that corpus. The
intersection $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ accepts iff both fire.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Pooled benchmark.} \textbf{CORD-v2}~\cite{park2019cord} test
($n{=}100$, labelled per-field amounts);
\textbf{SROIE Task-3}~\cite{huang2019sroie} canonical ($n{=}347$,
OCR-derived amounts);
\textbf{WildReceipt}~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt} test ($n{=}472$,
per-token annotations). Pooled $n{=}919$.

\noindent\textbf{Models.} Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} checkpoints
\texttt{naver-clova-ix/donut-base-finetuned-cord-v2} and
\texttt{philschmid/donut-base-sroie}; LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}
\texttt{Theivaprakasham/layoutlmv3-finetuned-wildreceipt}. Inference-only.

\noindent\textbf{Operating point.} $\varepsilon{=}0.02$ and
$k_{\min}{=}2$ unless stated otherwise.

\noindent\textbf{Statistics.} Per-cell precision is reported with
Wilson 95\% confidence intervals; agreement between the two gates is
tested with paired McNemar ($\chi^2$ with continuity correction).

\noindent\textbf{Softmax extraction across architectures.} For Donut the
softmax score is the geometric mean of per-token probabilities over
the predicted \texttt{total\_value} span in the decoder's
autoregressive output. For LayoutLMv3 it is the geometric mean of the
predicted-class probability on input tokens tagged
\texttt{Total\_value}. The two objects are architecture-specific
(decoder probabilities vs.\ token-classifier probabilities) but
conceptually analogous: each is the model's self-assessed probability
of its emitted total-value tokens. We treat them as the
architecture-appropriate ``model self-confidence,'' not as numerically
interchangeable.

McNemar asks the paired question: when the two gates disagree, do
they do so asymmetrically? A small $p$ would mean one gate strictly
dominates the other on \emph{which} examples it gets right, which
would weaken the case for intersection. The non-rejection on every
corpus is consistent with the gates being similarly accurate on
disjoint slices, and the WildReceipt cell, with $b{=}c{=}91$ exactly,
is the cleanest example: equal-size disjoint correct-accept slices
that combine into the $113/114{=}0.991$ intersection
(Fig.~\ref{fig:wildreceipt}). McNemar non-rejection is, strictly,
absence of evidence for asymmetry rather than positive evidence of
error-decorrelation; we list a direct correlation-on-errors test as
follow-up in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{F3: where $\sigma$ wins, ties, and loses}
\label{sec:results-regime}

Single-gate $\sigma$ precision varies across regimes: CORD $0.982$,
SROIE $0.867$, WildReceipt $0.953$. The pattern is regime-mechanistic.
On CORD, where $M$ comes from clean per-field labels, $\sigma$ beats
softmax outright by $5.5$ percentage points ($0.982$ vs.\ $0.927$); on
WildReceipt, where $M$ comes from per-token annotations, the two
gates tie at $0.953$; on SROIE, where $M$ is OCR-derived, $\sigma$
trails ($0.867$ vs.\ $0.947$). We attribute the SROIE loss to the
upstream OCR step, not to the verifier: digit confusions and phantom
amounts inflate $T(M)$ with values that pass as witnesses by
coincidence. A hand-OCR-corrected SROIE subsample is the controlled
test and remains future work. On every corpus, the intersection
still rises strictly above both single gates.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Why $\sigma$ wins, ties, and loses.} On corpora where
$M$ comes from reliable annotations (CORD JSON, WildReceipt per-token
labels), $\sigma$ either beats softmax outright or ties it. SROIE is
the sole inversion regime, and the cause is OCR-derived $M$, not the
verifier. In any deployment where $M$ can be read cleanly, $\sigma$
alone is competitive, and $\sigma{+}$softmax is dominant.

\noindent\textbf{Why the intersection wins even when singletons tie.}
On WildReceipt the two signals are equally precise alone yet their
intersection reaches $0.991$. Orthogonality doesn't require
dominance; it only requires \emph{errors on different inputs}.
McNemar non-significance on every corpus confirms this.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-architecture survival.} Swapping Donut
(generative) for LayoutLMv3 (encoder-only) keeps the intersection
above $0.95$. $\sigma$ is signal-orthogonal to softmax across
architectures, not just within a single backbone.

\noindent\textbf{Operational recipe.}
(1)~Pick any KIE model; get $\pi$ and softmax $s$ per receipt.
(2)~Extract $M$ (labelled fields if available; OCR + multi-candidate
$\tau$ otherwise).
(3)~Run $\sigma$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.02$, $k_{\min}{=}2$ on labelled
corpora and $k_{\min}{=}1$ on OCR.
(4)~Accept iff $\sigma{=}1$ \emph{and} $s\geq$ a fixed-coverage softmax
threshold. Defer otherwise.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

(i)~$\sigma$ requires arithmetic closure of the target field; it
applies to totals, subtotals, taxes, not vendor, date, address.
(ii)~We evaluate one field across two backbones and three corpora;
multi-field $\sigma$ is journal work.
(iii)~The cardinality-guard inversion is directly measured only on
SROIE; the CORD-side claim is structural.
(iv)~$\varepsilon{=}0.02$ assumes integer-cent inputs; jurisdictions
with fractional-cent rounding may need a different sweep.
(v)~$\sigma$ assumes $\pi$ is a parsed numeric scalar; format-hallucinated
model outputs (trailing currency strings, post-hoc tax annotations,
formatting noise) require an upstream normaliser before $\sigma$ can
verify them, and the paper does not benchmark that normaliser.
(vi)~Wilson and McNemar are appropriate at the per-corpus level; a
positive cross-method error-correlation test (Pearson $\phi$ between
gate error indicators with bootstrap CI) is the natural next-form
follow-up statistic on top of paired McNemar.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented $\sigma$, an arithmetic verifier for receipt total
extraction that ignores the model and asks the document directly
whether a subset of visible amounts sums to the prediction. On a
pooled three-corpus benchmark of $n{=}919$ receipts spanning a
generative and an encoder-only backbone, intersecting $\sigma$ with
matched-coverage softmax is right $160$ of $162$ times it accepts
(Wilson lower bound $0.956$), with both single gates below $0.92$;
McNemar tests are non-significant on every corpus, the WildReceipt
$b{=}c{=}91$ symmetry being the cleanest signature of disjoint errors.
Single-gate $\sigma$ tracks the cleanliness of the receipt amount
multiset: it beats softmax where the amounts are labelled, ties on
per-token annotations, and trails on OCR-derived inputs, with the
trail localisable to upstream OCR, not to the verifier. The cost is
microseconds per receipt, and precision is stable within
$[0.949, 0.980]$ under up to $40\%$ injected money-line noise. The
result the paper actually establishes is narrow: an independent
arithmetic check, when the receipt admits one, is precision-orthogonal
to softmax confidence, and the two signals are usefully combined.
