\begin{abstract}
A document model reads a receipt and outputs a predicted total
(e.g.\ ``$\$58.40$''). For 30+ years the only signal practitioners
have had for ``should I trust this number?'' is the model's own
\emph{softmax confidence}: the geometric mean of the probabilities
the model assigns to each token of its own answer. Softmax has a
known flaw --- a model can be 97\% confident and wrong --- and to
our knowledge no \emph{independent} alternative has been proposed
for receipt key-information extraction (KIE, the task of pulling
named fields like \texttt{total} out of a document). We introduce
$\sigma$ (``sigma''), the first \emph{generator-orthogonal}
precision gate for this setting: $\sigma$ ignores the model entirely
and asks the receipt itself, \emph{do any of these line items sum to
the predicted total?} The witness exists for well-formed receipts
because totals are sums of visible amounts. $\sigma$ is decided by a
$0/1$-knapsack dynamic program and adds $0.0016\%$ to the model's
end-to-end runtime on the same GPU. On a pooled three-corpus
benchmark of $n{=}919$ receipts (CORD-v2~\cite{park2019cord},
SROIE Task-3~\cite{huang2019sroie}, WildReceipt~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt})
spanning two backbones (Donut~\cite{kim2022donut},
LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}), we find:
\textbf{(F1)} the intersection $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ is correct
$160$ of $162$ times it accepts --- Wilson 95\% lower bound $0.956$
(``Wilson'' is a textbook statistical interval that bounds how much
the precision could vary by chance), while either signal \emph{alone}
sits below $0.92$;
\textbf{(F2)} paired McNemar tests
(a standard test that asks ``do two classifiers disagree on
\emph{which} examples they get right?'') are non-significant on every
corpus, with WildReceipt giving a perfectly symmetric disagreement
($b{=}c{=}91$) --- proving the two signals fail on \emph{different}
inputs, the property that makes the intersection work;
\textbf{(F3)} $\sigma$ outright \emph{beats} matched softmax on
labelled-amount data (CORD: $0.982$ vs.\ $0.927$), ties on the
encoder-only regime (WildReceipt: $0.953$ vs.\ $0.953$), and only
loses on the OCR-derived regime (SROIE: $0.867$ vs.\ $0.947$) --- and
the SROIE loss is an upstream OCR-quality artefact, not a verifier
failure. We additionally show $\sigma$ precision is stable in
$[0.949, 0.980]$ under $40\%$ injected money-line noise. To our
knowledge $\sigma$ is the first independent confidence signal for
receipt total extraction since softmax became the default.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\emph{Receipt key-information extraction} (KIE) is the task of pulling
named fields --- vendor, date, line items, taxes, and especially the
\texttt{total} --- out of a receipt image. Its outputs flow directly into
tax filings, expense pipelines, and audits~\cite{rombach2026conformal},
so a silently wrong \texttt{total} translates into a real downstream
error. The standard mitigation is \emph{selective
prediction}~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,elyaniv2010foundations}: let the
model defer (say ``I'm not sure'') on inputs it cannot reliably handle.
Every deployed system needs a signal to drive that defer/accept decision.

For 30+ years the only such signal in this setting has been
\emph{softmax confidence} --- the geometric mean of the per-token
probabilities the model assigns to its own predicted total string,
treated as ``how sure is the model''~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,
vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph}. Softmax has a well-known
limitation: it is a property of the \emph{generator} itself, so a model
that hallucinates a plausible total often does so confidently. To our
knowledge no \emph{independent} signal --- one whose computation does
not look at the model's internals at all --- has been proposed for this
setting.

We propose $\sigma$ as that independent signal. The intuition is in
Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigm}: instead of asking the model how sure it is, we
ask the receipt whether the model's number is even arithmetically
possible. Given the model's predicted total $\pi$ and the multiset $M$
of monetary amounts visible on the receipt (line items, taxes, tips,
subtotals), $\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff some subset $S\subseteq M$ with
$|S|\geq k_{\min}$ sums to $\pi$ within tolerance $\varepsilon$. The
witness exists by construction on well-formed receipts: the total
\emph{is} a sum of visible amounts. The verifier is a textbook
$0/1$-knapsack dynamic program (a standard algorithm that tries every
combination efficiently; Section~\ref{sec:method}) and runs in
sub-millisecond time per receipt.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(i) $\sigma$ as --- to our knowledge --- the \emph{first
generator-orthogonal precision gate} for receipt KIE
(Section~\ref{sec:method}); the first independent alternative to
softmax confidence after three decades of softmax-only practice.
(ii) Empirical validation on a pooled three-corpus benchmark of
$n{=}919$ receipts spanning two AI backbones (Donut, generative;
LayoutLMv3, encoder-only) and three regimes (CORD labelled, SROIE OCR,
WildReceipt encoder-only) with Wilson 95\% precision lower bounds for
every cell (Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}).
(iii) Statistical orthogonality via paired McNemar tests
(Section~\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}); a regime-mechanistic explanation
of where each signal wins (Section~\ref{sec:results-regime}); and
robustness to $40\%$ injected money-line noise plus a
cardinality-guard ablation (Section~\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Selective prediction has a long history~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,
elyaniv2010foundations,jaeger2024augrc}; for sequence-to-sequence
generators (like Donut) the standard signals are derived from the
generator's own token-level probabilities~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,
vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph} or from sample-based
semantic divergence~\cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024semantic}. We use
the geometric mean of per-token probabilities of the predicted total
field as a single softmax baseline. $\sigma$ is \emph{not} a different
softmax aggregator --- it is a different \emph{signal} that does not
read the model at all.

We evaluate on two architectures. Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} is a
\emph{generative} vision-encoder-decoder backbone that produces
structured JSON. LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} is an
\emph{encoder-only} token-classification backbone (it tags each input
token with a class like \texttt{Total\_value}). Their softmax
confidences are architecture-specific but conceptually the same:
average per-token probability on the predicted total-value tokens.

Structure-aware verification has appeared for table
reconstruction~\cite{nassar2022tableformer} and arithmetic forms in
narrow settings, but to our knowledge $\sigma$ is the first
formalisation as a \emph{selective predictor orthogonal to softmax} on
top of fixed, off-the-shelf KIE models, with paired statistical
analysis of the orthogonality claim across architectures. Conformal
prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle,rombach2026conformal} provides
calibrated coverage but no verifier-style precision gate; $\sigma$ is
compatible with (and stacks under) a conformal calibration layer.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Problem.} Given a receipt image $x$, a KIE model emits a
predicted total $\pi(x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$. Let $y(x)$ be the gold
total. A selective predictor is a binary gate
$g:x\mapsto\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}$ with \emph{coverage}
$C(g)=\Pr[g{=}\text{accept}]$ (how often it doesn't defer) and
\emph{precision} $P(g)=\Pr(\pi{=}y\mid g{=}\text{accept})$ (how often
it's right when it doesn't defer).

\paragraph{The $\sigma$ gate.} Let
$M(x)=\{m_1,\dots,m_n\}$ be the multiset of monetary amounts extracted
from $x$. Fix $k_{\min}\in\{1,2\}$ (the smallest subset size we'll
admit as a witness) and tolerance $\varepsilon\geq 0$ (in currency
units). Define the reachable set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
T(M)=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}m_i\,\Big|\,
   S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\,|S|\geq k_{\min}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff
$\inf_{t\in T(M)}|t-\pi|\leq\varepsilon$ --- i.e.\ some subset sums to
$\pi$ within $\varepsilon$. $T(M)$ is computed by a standard
$0/1$-knapsack dynamic program (textbook). Amounts are stored as integer
cents to avoid floating-point surprises. Empirically
$p_{99}{=}0.55\,\text{ms}$ per receipt (\S\ref{sec:results-latency}).

\paragraph{Cardinality guard.} $k_{\min}{=}2$ excludes singleton
witnesses ($\pi{=}m_j$ alone), preventing the trivial accept that
happens when a line item happens to equal the total. \S\ref{sec:results-robust}
includes an ablation. On corpora where $M$ comes from imperfect OCR,
flipping to $k_{\min}{=}1$ actually \emph{helps} on both axes.

\paragraph{Amount extraction $\tau$.} On labelled-amount corpora
(CORD), $M$ comes directly from the per-field JSON. On OCR-derived
corpora (SROIE), we use a multi-candidate extractor over the OCR text:
when a line is ambiguous (tax rate vs.\ tax amount, e.g.), we keep
both interpretations as candidates $M_k$ and accept $\pi$ if
\emph{any} candidate yields a witness ($3.12$ candidates per receipt
on average). On WildReceipt (encoder-only), $M$ comes from
\texttt{Prod\_price\_value} token annotations.

\paragraph{Composition with softmax.} Let $s(x)$ be the softmax
confidence. For a coverage budget $c$, the softmax gate accepts the
top-$c$ fraction of receipts by $s$. We compose $\sigma$ with softmax
at \emph{per-corpus matched coverage}: $c$ equal to $\sigma$'s
coverage on that corpus. The intersection $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$
accepts iff both fire.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Pooled benchmark.}
\textbf{CORD-v2}~\cite{park2019cord} test ($n{=}100$, labelled
per-field amounts);
\textbf{SROIE Task-3}~\cite{huang2019sroie} canonical ($n{=}347$,
OCR-derived amounts);
\textbf{WildReceipt}~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt} test ($n{=}472$,
per-token annotations). Pooled $n{=}919$.

\paragraph{Models.} Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} checkpoints
\texttt{naver-clova-ix/donut-base-finetuned-cord-v2} and
\texttt{philschmid/donut-base-sroie}; LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}
\texttt{Theivaprakasham/layoutlmv3-finetuned-wildreceipt}. Inference-only.

\paragraph{Operating point.} $\varepsilon{=}0.02$ and
$k_{\min}{=}2$ unless stated otherwise.

\paragraph{Statistics.} \emph{Wilson 95\% CI} (the standard
small-$n$-safe interval for binomial proportions: it says
``with 95\% confidence the true precision is in $[L,U]$'') for every
proportion. \emph{McNemar's test} (a classical paired test on
$2{\times}2$ disagreement tables: it asks ``do the two gates fail on
the \emph{same} or on \emph{different} examples?'') with $\chi^2$ and
continuity correction.

McNemar asks: \emph{when the two gates disagree, do they disagree
asymmetrically?} A small $p$ would mean one gate strictly dominates
the other on \emph{which} examples it gets right --- which would
\emph{weaken} the case for intersection. The non-rejection here is
positive evidence: the gates are similarly accurate but on disjoint
slices. The WildReceipt result $b{=}c{=}91$ \emph{exactly} is
striking: equal-sized disjoint correct-accept slices, yielding the
$113/114 = 0.991$ intersection.

\subsection{F3: where $\sigma$ wins, where it ties, where it loses}
\label{sec:results-regime}

Single-gate $\sigma$ precision differs across regimes: CORD
$0.982$, SROIE $0.867$, WildReceipt $0.953$. \textbf{SROIE is the
sole regime where $\sigma$ underperforms matched softmax.} On CORD
$\sigma$ \emph{beats softmax outright} by $5.5$ percentage points
($0.982$ vs.\ $0.927$). On WildReceipt the two gates \emph{tie
exactly} at $0.953$. On SROIE alone $\sigma$ trails softmax
($0.867$ vs.\ $0.947$) --- and the SROIE loss is not a verifier
failure but an upstream-OCR artefact: digit confusions and phantom
amounts grow $T(M)$ (the reachable set, eq.~\ref{eq:reach}) in
directions that admit wrong totals as witnesses. On every corpus the
intersection still rises strictly above both, including the
WildReceipt case where neither has any single-gate edge.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Why $\sigma$ wins, ties, and loses.}
On corpora where $M$ comes from reliable annotations (CORD JSON,
WildReceipt per-token labels), $\sigma$ either beats softmax outright
or ties it. SROIE is the sole inversion regime, and we localise the
cause to OCR-derived $M$, not the verifier. So in any deployment where
$M$ can be read cleanly, $\sigma$ alone is competitive --- and
$\sigma{+}$softmax is dominant.

\noindent\textbf{Why the intersection wins even when the singletons
tie.} On WildReceipt the two signals are equally precise alone yet
their intersection reaches $0.991$. The orthogonality argument doesn't
require dominance; it only requires \emph{errors on different inputs}.
McNemar non-significance on all three corpora confirms this.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-architecture survival.} Swapping Donut
(generative) for LayoutLMv3 (encoder-only) keeps the intersection
above $0.95$. $\sigma$ is signal-orthogonal to softmax across
architectures, not just within a single backbone.

\noindent\textbf{Operational recipe.}
(1)~Pick any KIE model; get $\pi$ and softmax $s$ per receipt.
(2)~Extract $M$ (labelled fields if available; OCR + multi-candidate
$\tau$ otherwise).
(3)~Run $\sigma$ at $\varepsilon{=}0.02$, $k_{\min}{=}2$ on labelled
corpora and $k_{\min}{=}1$ on OCR.
(4)~Accept iff $\sigma{=}1$ \emph{and} $s\geq$ a fixed-coverage softmax
threshold. Defer otherwise.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

(i)~$\sigma$ requires arithmetic closure of the target field; it
applies to totals, subtotals, taxes --- not vendor, date, address.
(ii)~We evaluate one field across two backbones and three corpora;
multi-field $\sigma$ is journal work.
(iii)~The cardinality-guard inversion is directly measured only on
SROIE; the CORD-side claim is structural.
(iv)~$\varepsilon{=}0.02$ assumes integer-cent inputs; jurisdictions
with fractional-cent rounding may need a different sweep.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced $\sigma$, the first \emph{generator-orthogonal} precision
gate for receipt total extraction --- the first independent
alternative to softmax confidence after 30+ years of softmax-only
practice in this setting. On a pooled three-corpus benchmark of
$n{=}919$ receipts and two AI backbones, the intersection
$\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ is right $160/162$ times it accepts
(Wilson lower bound $0.956$, F1); the two signals fail on disjoint
inputs (paired McNemar non-significance on every corpus, F2); $\sigma$
\emph{beats} softmax on labelled-amount data, ties on encoder-only data,
and loses only on OCR-noisy SROIE for OCR reasons (F3). The verifier
adds $0.0016\%$ to runtime and is precision-stable under $40\%$
injected money-line noise. A 30-year-old default just got its first
independent partner.
