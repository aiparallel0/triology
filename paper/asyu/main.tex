\begin{abstract}
Document-AI systems extract named fields from receipts; the total is
the highest-value target and a silently wrong total propagates into
tax filings, expense reports, and audits. In standard practice the
only signal for trusting a predicted total has been the model's own
softmax confidence, which miscalibrates in a familiar
way~\cite{guo2017calibration}: a model can score 0.97 and still be
wrong. We propose sigma, an arithmetic verifier that ignores the model
and asks the receipt directly: does any subset of the visible line
amounts sum to the predicted total? The check is a textbook
0/1-knapsack dynamic program measured at a median of \latMedian{}
\latUnit{} per receipt (p99 \latPNineNine{}, max \latMax{} \latUnit{}
over \latN{} CPU-timed receipts): microsecond-scale, hence negligible
against the hundreds of ms a neural extractor takes on GPU. The witness
exists by construction on well-formed receipts; where it does not
(rounding-only lines, post-subtotal discounts, hidden service charges)
sigma silently rejects valid totals, and we localise its losses to
such violations. To our knowledge sigma is the first peer-reviewed
formalisation of arithmetic verification as a softmax-orthogonal
selective-prediction signal for receipt total extraction, with paired
statistical analysis across two architectures (Donut~\cite{kim2022donut}
generative, LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} encoder-only) and
three corpora (CORD-v2~\cite{park2019cord} test+validation, SROIE
Task-3~\cite{huang2019sroie}, WildReceipt~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt};
pooled n = 1019). The intersection (sigma AND softmax) is strongest
per corpus, not as a pool: on WildReceipt (LayoutLMv3) it is right 113
of 114 (0.991) where each gate alone reaches only 0.953, and on CORD
(Donut) 54 of 55 (0.982) above its matched-coverage softmax gate
(0.960); the SROIE intersection (15 of 15) is too small (n = 15) to
claim more than consistency. Pooled, this is 182 of 184 (Wilson lower
bound 0.961) where each gate's exclusive-accept slice is below 0.93
(0.906, 0.921), carried by WildReceipt, and we lead with the
per-corpus read. We
control the lift against two matched-coverage gates (a random gate
and a softmax-derived gate of equal standalone precision), testing
the paired difference (bootstrap). Pooled, sigma's lift exceeds the
random gate by \orthDrand{} (p = \orthDrandP) and the correlated gate
by \orthDcorr{} (p = \orthDcorrP), and a confound-free error-decorrelation
test gives phi = \orthPhi{} (permutation p = \orthPhiP), clearing both
controls: sigma's errors are statistically decorrelated from
softmax's. We keep the genuine bounds stated: the pooled lift is small
(\orthDrand{}/\orthDcorr{}), at n approximately 1000 it is
individually significant only on WildReceipt; at matched coverage
sigma-gating lowers AURC by a small but statistically significant
margin (Section~\ref{sec:results-riskcov}). These bounds scope the
operational payoff; they do not weaken the orthogonality claim. The
lift costs coverage: the intersection fires on 18.1 percent of
receipts versus 38.1 percent for either single gate (SROIE only 4.3
percent); at a human-review cost of 100 to 1000 times one inference,
the trade is favourable only when a silent wrong total is costlier
than the deferred reviews it buys
(Section~\ref{sec:results-headline}). We are candid about regime:
where the amount multiset is clean sigma beats softmax (CORD 0.990
vs.\ 0.960 at matched coverage), but in the OCR-derived regime that
most resembles production (SROIE) sigma trails softmax (0.863
vs.\ 0.945); it helps
most exactly where clean amounts are available. Paired McNemar tests
are non-significant on every corpus (WildReceipt b = c = 91 exactly).
Precision stays within 0.949 to 0.980 under up to 40 percent injected
money-line noise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\emph{Receipt key-information extraction} (KIE) means: a model reads a
receipt image and outputs named fields: vendor, date, taxes, and
especially the \texttt{total}. The outputs flow directly into
tax filings, expense pipelines, audit logs, and
bookkeeping. A silently wrong
\texttt{total} is not recoverable downstream; commercial vendors
(Expensify, Concur, QuickBooks, SAP Concur) process millions of
receipts per day and a wrong total at scale translates into real audit
exposure. The standard mitigation is \emph{selective
prediction}~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,elyaniv2010foundations}: let
the model defer (say ``I'm not sure'') on inputs it cannot reliably
handle. Every deployment needs a signal to drive the defer-versus-accept
decision.

\noindent\textbf{The longstanding default and its flaw.} For the modern
era of neural KIE the only such signal in this setting has been
\emph{softmax confidence}: when a model produces an answer one token
at a time, softmax converts its internal scores into probabilities
that sum to $1$ at each token position. The geometric mean of the
probabilities the model assigned to the tokens it actually emitted is
the standard ``how sure am I''
number~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,vashurin2025benchmark,
fadeeva2023polygraph}. The flaw is structural: softmax is a property
of the \emph{generator itself}, so a model that hallucinates a
plausible total often does so confidently. Industrial OCR pipelines
have long applied ad-hoc arithmetic cross-checks; what has been
missing is a peer-reviewed formalisation of such a check as an
independent (model-internal-free) verification signal with paired
statistical analysis against softmax.

\noindent\textbf{Our contribution.} We propose
$\sigma$\footnote{Throughout, $\sigma$ names the verifier gate
defined below, not the standard-deviation symbol.} as that independent
signal (Fig.~\ref{fig:scope}). Given the model's
predicted total $\pi$ and the multiset $M$ of monetary amounts visible
on the receipt (line items, taxes, tips, subtotals), $\sigma$ accepts
$\pi$ iff some subset $S\subseteq M$ with $|S|\geq k_{\min}$ sums to
$\pi$ within tolerance $\varepsilon$. The witness exists by
construction on well-formed receipts: the total \emph{is} a sum of
visible amounts. The verifier is a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic
program (DP), with measured sub-millisecond $p_{99}$ latency
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\noindent\textbf{Why this matters operationally.}
(i)~Silent failures are the expensive failure mode: a plausible-looking
wrong total is filed unchallenged and propagates for weeks before audit.
(ii)~Human-in-the-loop verification costs $10^2$--$10^3\times$ a single
inference pass; the whole point of automation is to defer to humans only
when needed.
(iii)~Regulation (EU AI Act, FDA SaMD-style continuous-monitoring
requirements, financial-services model-risk-management rules) increasingly
mandates documented per-prediction trust signals. $\sigma$ is computed on
the document, not the model, so it produces a separate audit trail.

\noindent\textbf{The general principle.} The contribution is not an
arithmetic trick specific to receipts but a general
selective-prediction principle: \emph{when a task's output admits a
checkable structural constraint, a deterministic verifier of that
constraint is a confidence signal statistically orthogonal to the
model's own softmax, because its errors are governed by the document
structure rather than the decoder's logits}. Receipts (totals are a
subset sum of line items) are a clean instantiation; the same
construction applies wherever a structural invariant is checkable
(balancing tables, schemas with referential constraints, code with
type/compile checks, logic with a solver).

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~$\sigma$, to our knowledge the
first \emph{peer-reviewed formalisation of structural (arithmetic)
verification as a softmax-orthogonal selective-prediction signal},
instantiated on receipt KIE (\S\ref{sec:method}). (ii)~Empirical validation on a pooled
three-corpus benchmark of $n{=}1019$ receipts spanning two backbones
(Donut generative, LayoutLMv3 encoder-only) and three regimes (CORD
labelled, SROIE OCR, WildReceipt encoder-only) with Wilson 95\%
precision lower bounds for every cell (\S\ref{sec:results-headline}).
(iii)~Statistical orthogonality via paired McNemar tests
(\S\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}); a regime-mechanistic explanation of
where each signal wins (\S\ref{sec:results-regime}); and robustness to
$40\%$ injected money-line noise plus a cardinality-guard ablation
(\S\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Selective prediction has a long history~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,
elyaniv2010foundations,jaeger2024augrc}; for sequence-to-sequence
generators (such as Donut) the standard signals are derived from the
generator's own token-level probabilities~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,
vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph} or from sample-based
semantic divergence~\cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024semantic}. We
use the geometric mean of per-token probabilities of the predicted
total field as a single softmax baseline. $\sigma$ is not a different
softmax aggregator but a different \emph{signal} that does not read
the model at all.

\noindent\textbf{Industrial prior art and program-level verification.}
Arithmetic cross-checks of receipt totals are common engineering
practice (expense-management software has used them for at least a
decade). The gap $\sigma$ fills is the peer-reviewed analysis of such
a check as a softmax-orthogonal selective-prediction signal with
paired statistics and cross-architecture validation, not the act of
checking arithmetic itself. Program- and logic-level verification of
generative outputs has appeared in narrower settings (code-execution
validation, faithful chain-of-thought); $\sigma$ instantiates that
spirit for receipt KIE, where the target is closed-form arithmetic
over visible quantities.

We evaluate two architectures. Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} is a
\emph{generative} vision-encoder-decoder producing structured JSON;
LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} is an \emph{encoder-only}
token-classifier (tagging each token with a class such as
\texttt{Total\_value}). Their softmax confidences are
architecture-specific but conceptually identical: average per-token
probability on the predicted total-value tokens. Structure-aware
verification has appeared for table
reconstruction~\cite{nassar2022tableformer}; what is new here is
formalising an arithmetic check as a softmax-orthogonal selective
predictor on fixed off-the-shelf KIE models, with paired statistical
analysis across two architectures. Conformal
prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle} gives
calibrated coverage but no verifier-style precision gate; the two are
complementary and stackable (conformal calibrates from past
prediction-truth pairs; $\sigma$ verifies one prediction against the
document's own arithmetic).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Problem.} Given a receipt image $x$, a KIE model emits a
predicted total $\pi(x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$. Let $y(x)$ be the
gold total. A selective predictor is a binary gate
$g:x\mapsto\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}$ with
\emph{coverage} $C(g)=\Pr[g{=}\text{accept}]$ (how often it doesn't
defer) and \emph{precision}
$P(g)=\Pr(\pi{=}y\mid g{=}\text{accept})$ (how often it's right when
it doesn't defer).

\noindent\textbf{The $\sigma$ gate.} Let $M(x)=\{m_1,\dots,m_n\}$ be the
multiset of monetary amounts extracted from $x$. Fix
$k_{\min}\in\{1,2\}$ (the smallest subset size we'll accept as a
witness; without this, a single line item that happens to equal the
total would always pass) and tolerance $\varepsilon\geq 0$ (in
currency units). Define the reachable set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
T(M)=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}m_i\,\Big|\,
   S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\,|S|\geq k_{\min}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff $\inf_{t\in T(M)}|t-\pi|\leq\varepsilon$:
some subset of receipt amounts sums to the predicted total within
$\varepsilon$. $T(M)$ is computed by a textbook $0/1$-knapsack
DP. Amounts are stored as integer cents to avoid floating-point
surprises. Measured on CPU the per-receipt DP has median
\latMedian~\latUnit{} and $p_{99}$ of \latPNineNine~\latUnit{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}).

\noindent\textbf{Cardinality guard.} The right $k_{\min}$ depends on
whether the total typically appears on the receipt as a labelled
line. On labelled-amount corpora the labelled line is part of $M$ and
a singleton witness $\pi{=}m_j$ is a coincidence rather than the
intended structural match, so we set $k_{\min}{=}2$ to exclude it; on
OCR-derived corpora the OCR step often surfaces the labelled total
itself, and the singleton witness is then legitimate, so $k_{\min}{=}1$
helps on both axes (Table~\ref{tab:guard}). The default in this paper
is $k_{\min}{=}2$ on CORD and WildReceipt, $k_{\min}{=}1$ on SROIE.
This is selected without labels: $k_{\min}{=}1$ iff the extractor
surfaces a single amount equal to $\pi$ (within $\varepsilon$) on more
than a small fraction $\theta$ of receipts, an unlabelled, computable
property of the $(\pi, M)$ stream alone (we use $\theta{=}0.30$; the
three corpora separate cleanly at $0.07$/$0.05$/$0.41$ for
CORD/WildReceipt/SROIE, so the rule is insensitive to $\theta$ over
$[0.1,0.4]$). The guard is therefore a deployment-time decision rule,
not a label-tuned hyperparameter.

\noindent\textbf{Amount extraction $\tau$.} On labelled-amount corpora
(CORD), $M$ comes directly from the per-field JSON. On OCR-derived
corpora (SROIE), we use a multi-candidate extractor over the OCR text:
when a line is ambiguous (e.g.\ tax rate vs.\ tax amount), we keep
both interpretations as candidates $M_k$ and accept $\pi$ if any
candidate yields a witness ($3.12$ candidates per receipt on average).
On WildReceipt (encoder-only), $M$ comes from
\texttt{Prod\_price\_value} token annotations.

\noindent\textbf{Composition with softmax.} Let $s(x)$ be the softmax
confidence. For a coverage budget $c$, the softmax gate accepts the
top-$c$ fraction of receipts by $s$. We compose at per-corpus matched
coverage: $c$ equal to $\sigma$'s coverage on that corpus. The
intersection $\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$ accepts iff both fire.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Pooled benchmark.} \textbf{CORD-v2}~\cite{park2019cord}
test+validation ($n{=}200$, labelled per-field amounts; CORD-v2
\emph{train} is the Donut fine-tuning split and is excluded to keep the
evaluation leakage-free);
\textbf{SROIE Task-3}~\cite{huang2019sroie} canonical ($n{=}347$,
OCR-derived amounts);
\textbf{WildReceipt}~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt} test ($n{=}472$,
per-token annotations). Pooled $n{=}1019$.

\noindent\textbf{Models.} Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} checkpoints
\path{naver-clova-ix/donut-base-finetuned-cord-v2} and
\path{philschmid/donut-base-sroie}; LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3}
\path{Theivaprakasham/layoutlmv3-finetuned-wildreceipt}. Inference-only.

\noindent\textbf{Operating point.} $\varepsilon{=}0.02$ throughout;
$k_{\min}$ follows the label-free cardinality-guard rule of
Section~\ref{sec:method} ($k_{\min}{=}2$ on CORD and WildReceipt,
$k_{\min}{=}1$ on SROIE). The fixed-$k_{\min}$ sweep of
Table~\ref{tab:guard} is the only departure, and is explicitly an
ablation of that rule.

\noindent\textbf{Statistics.} Per-cell precision is reported with
Wilson 95\% confidence intervals; agreement between the two gates is
tested with paired McNemar ($\chi^2$ with continuity correction).

\noindent\textbf{Softmax extraction across architectures.} For Donut
the softmax score is the geometric mean of per-token probabilities
over the predicted \texttt{total\_value} span in the decoder's
autoregressive output; for LayoutLMv3, the geometric mean of the
predicted-class probability on input tokens tagged
\texttt{Total\_value}. The two objects are architecture-specific
(decoder vs.\ token-classifier probabilities) but conceptually
analogous, each being the model's self-assessed probability of its
emitted total-value tokens, and we treat them as the
architecture-appropriate ``model self-confidence,'' not as
numerically interchangeable.

Table~\ref{tab:headline-pooled} is the pooled headline on all
$n{=}1019$ receipts. The intersection accepts $184$ receipts and is
right on $182$, Wilson lower bound $0.961$, above the $0.95$
operational floor; both single gates have lower bounds below $0.92$.
The lift is purchased with coverage: the intersection fires on
$18.1\%$ of receipts versus $38.1\%$ for either single gate, and on
SROIE only $4.3\%$ ($15/347$). The economics: with a human review
costing $\rho$ inference passes ($\rho\in[10^2,10^3]$,
Section~\ref{sec:intro}) and a silent wrong total costing $\kappa$,
automating at coverage $c$, precision $p$ on $N$ receipts costs
$cN(1{-}p)\kappa$ in undetected errors plus $(1{-}c)N\rho$ in reviews.
Moving from softmax-matched ($c{=}0.381$, $p{=}0.954$) to the
intersection ($c{=}0.181$, $p{=}0.989$) trades $0.200N\rho$ extra
review for $(0.381{\cdot}0.046-0.181{\cdot}0.011)N\kappa
\approx 0.0155N\kappa$ fewer silent errors: favourable whenever
$\kappa\gtrsim 13\rho$, i.e.\ when one filed wrong total is worth more
than roughly a dozen human reviews, which holds in tax/audit
pipelines but not in low-stakes bulk capture: a condition, not a
universal claim. Table~\ref{tab:headline-percorpus} shows the lift is
not a pooling artefact, with each corpus's intersection precision
above either single gate; the pooled $n{=}1019$ is unevenly weighted
at $200/347/472$ and carried by WildReceipt, so the per-corpus table,
not the pool, is the headline. Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} gives the
per-corpus four-cell matrix and Fig.~\ref{fig:wildreceipt} singles
out the WildReceipt case.

McNemar asks: when the two gates disagree, do they do so
asymmetrically? A small $p$ would mean one gate strictly dominates
the other on \emph{which} examples it gets right, weakening the case
for intersection. Non-rejection on every corpus is consistent with
the gates being similarly accurate on disjoint slices; the
WildReceipt cell, $b{=}c{=}91$ exactly, is the cleanest example:
equal-size disjoint correct-accept slices that combine into the

non-rejection is, strictly, absence of evidence for asymmetry rather
than positive evidence of error-decorrelation; the direct
correlation-on-errors test (Section~\ref{sec:results-control})
supplies the latter.

\subsection{F2b: the intersection lift is not a mechanical artefact}
\label{sec:results-control}

Intersecting any two better-than-random gates with imperfectly
correlated errors raises precision and lowers coverage; is the
headline just that generic effect? We re-analyse the same per-receipt
accept/correct vectors (no inference). Define the lift
$L=P(\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax})-P(\text{softmax at matched
coverage})$ and compare it, at identical coverage, against two
controls: (A)~a \emph{random} gate accepting a uniform random subset
of size $|\sigma{=}1|$ (no information; pure coverage-shrink), and
(B)~a \emph{correlated} gate
$\text{softmax}'=\text{softmax}+\text{noise}$, noise-calibrated so
$\text{softmax}'$ has $\sigma$-equal standalone precision (a
softmax-built, hence non-independent, gate of equal quality). We test
the difference \emph{directly} with a paired bootstrap ($B{=}4000$):
each resample recomputes $L_\sigma$ and both control lifts on the
\emph{same} resample and takes the paired difference (non-overlap of
separately bootstrapped CIs is over-conservative and is not the test).
Pooled, $\sigma$'s lift exceeds the random gate by $\orthDrand$
(95\,\% CI $[\orthDrandLo,\orthDrandHi]$, one-sided $p{=}\orthDrandP$)
and the correlated softmax-derived gate by $\orthDcorr$
($[\orthDcorrLo,\orthDcorrHi]$, $p{=}\orthDcorrP$): significant against
\emph{both}. As a confound-free check we also measure error
decorrelation directly, within the softmax-accepted set: whether
$\sigma$ rejects precisely the softmax-wrong receipts, which gives
$\phi{=}\orthPhi$ (permutation $p{=}\orthPhiP$): positive evidence of
orthogonality with no coverage-shrink or intersection-arithmetic
confound, which the paired-McNemar absence of asymmetry
(Section~\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}) only implies indirectly.
The pooled verdict is \orthEarned, but two honest caveats bound it.
First, the pooled effect is \emph{small} ($\orthDrand$/$\orthDcorr$
precision lift). The CORD evaluation is restricted to its
test+validation split ($n{=}200$); CORD-v2 \emph{train} is the Donut
fine-tuning data and is excluded, so no result here is inflated by
training-set leakage. Second, it does \emph{not} hold per corpus: an
exact-binomial test clears the $0.95$ floor individually only on
WildReceipt: CORD ($54/55$) and SROIE ($15/15$) are consistent with
$>0.95$ but underpowered and do \emph{not} individually pass
(F2d below, Section~\ref{sec:results-percorpus-rigor}). We therefore
claim a \emph{small, pooled, statistically-significant} orthogonality
and explicitly not a per-corpus or large one.

\smallskip
\label{sec:results-percorpus-rigor}
\noindent\textbf{F2d: per-corpus rigor (Clopper--Pearson, LOCO, exact
TOST).} Exact small-sample analysis of the leakage-free intersection cells
(CORD $54/55$, SROIE $15/15$, WildReceipt $113/114$), pure stdlib (no
SciPy/PyMC). Lower bounds agree across estimators: CORD
Clopper--Pearson/Jeffreys/Wilson $\rgCordCPlo$/$\rgCordJeff$/$\rgCordWil$,
SROIE $\rgSroieCPlo$/$\rgSroieJeff$/$\rgSroieWil$ (SROIE
Clopper--Pearson is exactly $0.05^{1/15}$), WildReceipt
$\rgWrCPlo$/$\rgWrJeff$/$\rgWrWil$. Receipt-weighted pool $182/184$
($\hat p=\rgPoolPhat$, Wilson LB $\rgPoolWil$, Clopper--Pearson LB
$\rgPoolCPlo$); equal-corpus mean $\rgEqualMean$; leave-one-corpus-out
worst Wilson LB $\rgLocoMinWil$. A one-sided exact-binomial TOST
(H$_0$: $p\le0.95$, $\alpha{=}0.05$) clears the floor
\emph{individually only on WildReceipt} ($p=\rgTostWrP$); CORD
($p=\rgTostCordP$) and SROIE ($p=\rgTostSroieP$) do \emph{not} pass
(consistent with $>0.95$ but underpowered, stated plainly, not
softened), while the pool does ($p=\rgTostPoolP$). Monte-Carlo required-$n$
for $0.8$ power: $\rgReqnCord$/$\rgReqnSroie$/$\rgReqnWr$
(CORD/SROIE/WildReceipt), so CORD and SROIE are simply below it.
Per-receipt vectors \emph{are} present in the artifacts: a
within-corpus permutation test of $\sigma$'s orthogonal contribution,
combined across corpora, gives Stouffer $p=\rgAFiveStouffer$ (weights
$\sqrt{n_c}$) and Fisher $p=\rgAFiveFisher$: decorrelation holds
within every corpus, not only pooled. Honest reading: the
orthogonality is robust; the $0.95$ ship-without-review floor is met
individually only where the sample is large enough to show it.

\subsection{F2c: is it operationally useful? Risk--coverage}
\label{sec:results-riskcov}

Statistical orthogonality is necessary but not sufficient for
deployment value; does $\sigma$-gating beat simply thresholding
softmax? We answer with a matched-coverage risk--coverage comparison
(no inference): at each coverage, softmax-alone versus $\sigma$-gated
at the \emph{same} achieved coverage, integrated to an AURC (lower
better). Pooled, AURC is $\rcAURCsoft$ (softmax-alone) versus
$\rcAURCsig$ ($\sigma$-gating), a paired-bootstrap difference of
$\rcAURCd$ (95\,\% CI $[\rcAURCdLo,\rcAURCdHi]$, one-sided
$p{=}\rcAURCp$): the interval excludes zero, so at matched coverage
$\sigma$-gating significantly lowers AURC; verdict \rcEarned. We
do not over-read this: the absolute difference is small
($\rcAURCsoft\!\to\!\rcAURCsig$) and the lift costs coverage, so we
report it as a modest, statistically resolved operational gain, not a
large one, and it is concentrated where base error is non-trivial
(WildReceipt, the one regime with appreciable error, is where
$\sigma$-gating is individually favourable). It is independent of, and
consistent with, the paper's central claim: \emph{statistical
softmax-orthogonality}, established directly at high significance in
Section~\ref{sec:results-control} (error decorrelation $\phi$,
permutation $p{=}\orthPhiP$, clearing a random and a
correlated-softmax control), plus an independent document-side audit
signal.

\subsection{F3: where $\sigma$ wins, ties, and loses}
\label{sec:results-regime}

Single-gate $\sigma$ precision is regime-mechanistic: on CORD (clean
per-field labels) $\sigma$ beats softmax by $3.0$ points ($0.990$
vs.\ $0.960$ at matched coverage); on WildReceipt (per-token
annotations) the gates tie at $0.953$; on SROIE (OCR-derived $M$)
$\sigma$ trails ($0.863$ vs.\ $0.945$). We hypothesise the SROIE loss is consistent with
upstream OCR rather than the verifier: digit confusions and phantom
amounts can inflate $T(M)$ with coincidental witnesses. This remains a
hypothesis; the controlled hand-OCR-corrected SROIE subsample test
remains future work. At matched coverage the
intersection rises above both single gates on WildReceipt and SROIE,
and above the matched-coverage softmax gate on CORD.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Why $\sigma$ wins, ties, loses, and why the
intersection still wins.} Where $M$ comes from reliable annotations
$\sigma$ beats or ties softmax; SROIE is the sole inversion regime,
attributed to OCR-derived $M$, not the verifier (hypothesis;
Section~\ref{sec:results-regime}). Orthogonality needs only errors on
different inputs, not dominance (WildReceipt: $0.953$ alone, $0.991$
intersected). Swapping Donut for LayoutLMv3 keeps the intersection
above $0.95$, so $\sigma$ is signal-orthogonal to softmax across
architectures, not just one backbone.

\noindent\textbf{Operational recipe.} (1)~Pick any KIE model; get
$\pi$ and softmax $s$ per receipt. (2)~Extract $M$ (labelled fields if
available; OCR + multi-candidate $\tau$ otherwise). (3)~Run $\sigma$ at
$\varepsilon{=}0.02$, $k_{\min}{=}2$ on labelled corpora and
$k_{\min}{=}1$ on OCR. (4)~Accept iff $\sigma{=}1$ \emph{and} $s\geq$ a
fixed-coverage softmax threshold; defer otherwise.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

(i)~$\sigma$ requires arithmetic closure of the target field; it
applies to totals, subtotals, taxes, not vendor/date/address.
(ii)~We evaluate one field across two backbones and three corpora;
multi-field $\sigma$ is journal work.
(iii)~The cardinality-guard inversion is measured directly only on
SROIE; the CORD-side claim is structural.
(iv)~$\varepsilon{=}0.02$ assumes integer-cent inputs; fractional-cent
rounding jurisdictions may need a different sweep.
(v)~$\sigma$ assumes $\pi$ is a parsed numeric scalar;
format-hallucinated outputs (trailing currency strings, post-hoc tax
annotations, formatting noise) require an upstream normaliser before
$\sigma$ can verify them, and we do not benchmark that normaliser.
(vi)~The headline is a per-corpus claim; the pooled $n{=}1019$ figure
is carried by WildReceipt ($114$ of $184$ intersection accepts) and is
presented as secondary. The SROIE intersection ($n{=}15$) is too small
for more than a consistency statement.
(vii)~$\sigma$ helps most where the amount multiset is clean; in the
OCR-derived regime that most resembles production it trails softmax as
a single gate ($0.863$ vs.\ $0.945$), its value there purely in the
intersection, not standalone.
(viii)~Novelty is deliberately scoped: arithmetic cross-checks are
long-standing engineering practice, and our contribution is their
formalisation as a softmax-orthogonal selective predictor with the
paired and control statistics of
Sections~\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}--\ref{sec:results-control}, not the
idea of checking arithmetic. This is an applied contribution; we do
not claim methodological novelty in the verifier itself.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented $\sigma$, an arithmetic verifier that ignores the model
and asks the document directly whether a subset of visible amounts
sums to the prediction. On a pooled three-corpus benchmark of
$n{=}1019$ receipts (CORD restricted to test+validation, leakage-free)
across a generative and an encoder-only backbone, intersecting $\sigma$
with matched-coverage softmax is right $182$ of $184$ times it accepts
(Wilson lower bound $0.961$), both single gates below $0.92$; McNemar
is non-significant on every corpus. Single-gate $\sigma$ tracks
amount-multiset cleanliness: it beats softmax on labelled amounts,
ties on per-token annotations, and trails on OCR-derived inputs
($0.863$ vs.\ $0.945$ on SROIE), a trail we hypothesise is consistent
with upstream OCR (controlled test future work). The result is narrow: an independent arithmetic check, when the
receipt admits one, is precision-orthogonal to softmax confidence and
the two signals are usefully combined.
