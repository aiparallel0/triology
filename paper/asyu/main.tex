\begin{abstract}
Document-AI systems frequently extract itemized fields from receipts, where the ``total'' field serves as the highest-value target. Errors in extracting this specific field can severely propagate into downstream processes like tax filings, expense reports, and financial audits. In standard practice, the softmax layers in Document-AI models often output miscalibrated predictions~\cite{guo2017calibration}, failing to detect these errors in isolation. To remedy this issue, we propose sigma-verifier, a novel validation layer that frames verification as a 0/1-knapsack problem, checking whether any subset of extracted itemized lines sums exactly to the predicted total. We evaluated the impact of the sigma-verifier, on receipt-parsing systems utilizing two prominent architectures Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} and LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} across various benchmark databases. Our empirical results demonstrate that the sigma-verifier yields up to a 77\% relative reduction in the silent-wrong-total rate among accepted receipts and reduces downstream detection errors by a factor of 4.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\emph{Receipt key-information extraction} (KIE) means: a model reads a
receipt image and outputs named fields: vendor, date, taxes, and
especially the \texttt{total}. The outputs flow directly into
tax filings, expense pipelines, audit logs, and
bookkeeping. A silently wrong
\texttt{total} is not recoverable downstream; commercial vendors
(Expensify, QuickBooks, SAP Concur) process millions of
receipts per day and a wrong total at scale translates into major failure. The standard mitigation is \emph{selective
prediction}~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,elyaniv2010foundations}: let
the model defer (say ``I'm not sure'') on inputs it cannot reliably
handle. Every deployment needs a signal to drive the defer-versus-accept
decision.

\noindent\textbf{The longstanding default and its flaw.} For the modern
era of neural KIE the only such signal in this setting has been
\emph{softmax confidence}: when a model produces an answer one token
at a time, softmax converts its internal scores into probabilities
that sum to $1$ at each token position. The geometric mean of the
probabilities the model assigned to the tokens it actually emitted is
the standard ``how sure am I''
number~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,vashurin2025benchmark,
fadeeva2023polygraph}. The flaw is structural: softmax is a property
of the \emph{generator itself}, so a model that hallucinates a
probable total often does so confidently. Industrial OCR pipelines
have long applied post-detection arithmetic cross-checks; what has been
missing is a peer-reviewed formalisation of such a check as an
independent (model-internal-free) verification signal with paired
statistical analysis against softmax.

\noindent\textbf{Our contribution.} We propose
$\sigma$\footnote{Throughout, $\sigma$ names the verifier
defined below, not the standard-deviation symbol.} as that independent
signal (Fig.~\ref{fig:scope}). Given the model's
predicted total $\pi$ and the multiset $M$ of monetary amounts visible
on the receipt (line items, taxes, tips, subtotals), $\sigma$ accepts
$\pi$ if some subset $S\subseteq M$ with $|S|\geq k_{\min}$ sums to
$\pi$ within tolerance $\varepsilon$. The witness exists by
construction on well-formed receipts: the total \emph{is} a sum of
visible amounts. The verifier is a textbook $0/1$-knapsack dynamic
program (DP), with measured sub-millisecond $p_{99}$ latency
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-robust}).

\noindent\textbf{In plain terms.} The sigma verifier reads the
receipt the same way a careful clerk would: it lists the monetary
numbers it can see (line prices, taxes, tips, subtotals) and asks
whether any subset of those numbers adds up to the total the model
reported. If yes, the receipt passes; if no, it is flagged. The check
looks at the document, not at the model's internal scores, so it
cannot be fooled by an overconfident prediction. We then accept a
receipt only when this structural decision rule and the model's own
softmax decision rule both fire.

\noindent\textbf{Why this matters operationally.}
(i)~Silent failures are the expensive failure mode: a possible-looking
wrong total is filed unchallenged and propagates for weeks before audit.
(ii)~Human-in-the-loop verification costs $10^2$--$10^3\times$ a single
inference pass; the whole point of automation is to defer to humans only
when needed.
(iii)~Regulation (EU AI Act, FDA SaMD-style continuous-monitoring
requirements, financial-services model-risk-management rules) increasingly
mandates documented per-prediction trust signals. $\sigma$ is computed on
the document, not the model, so it produces a separate audit trail.

\noindent\textbf{The general principle.} The contribution is not an
arithmetic trick specific to receipts but a general
selective-prediction principle: \emph{when a task's output admits a
checkable structural constraint, a deterministic verifier of that
constraint is a confidence signal statistically orthogonal to the
model's own softmax, because its errors are governed by the document
structure rather than the decoder's logits}. Receipts (totals are a
subset sum of line items) are a clean instantiation; the same
construction applies wherever a structural invariant is checkable
(balancing tables, schemas with referential constraints, code with
type/compile checks, logic with a solver).

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} (i)~$\sigma$, to our knowledge the
first \emph{peer-reviewed formalisation of structural (arithmetic)
verification as a softmax-orthogonal selective-prediction signal},
instantiated on receipt KIE (\S\ref{sec:method}). ii) We validate our approach empirically on a pooled benchmark of 1,019 receipts drawn from three datasets, tested across two AI architectures Donut (a generative model) and LayoutLMv3 (an encoder-only classifier) and three different receipt data regimes. For every reported result, we provide Wilson 95\% confidence interval lower bounds to ensure the precision estimates are statistically rigorous rather than point estimates that could be misleading on small samples.
iii)We show that σ (the arithmetic verifier) and softmax confidence make their mistakes on different receipts they do not fail together. Using the McNemar test, we confirm that the errors of these two methods are statistically independent, and it is precisely this independence that makes combining them so powerful.
We further show that the arithmetic check remains reliable even when up to 40\% of receipt line items are corrupted with injected noise. We also confirm through ablation that the cardinality guard the rule requiring at least two line items to sum to the predicted total, rather than permitting a single coincidental match — is a necessary design choice that meaningfully improves precision.


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Selective prediction has a long history~\cite{geifman2017selectivenet,
elyaniv2010foundations,jaeger2024augrc}; for sequence-to-sequence
generators (such as Donut) the standard signals are derived from the
generator's own token-level probabilities~\cite{malinin2021uncertainty,
vashurin2025benchmark,fadeeva2023polygraph} or from sample-based
semantic divergence~\cite{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024semantic}. We
use the geometric mean of per-token probabilities of the predicted
total field as a single softmax baseline. $\sigma$ is not a different
softmax aggregator but a different \emph{signal} that does not read
the model at all.

\noindent\textbf{Industrial prior art and program-level verification.}
Arithmetic cross-checks of receipt totals are common engineering
practice (expense-management software has used them for at least a
decade). The gap $\sigma$ fills is the peer-reviewed analysis of such
a check as a softmax-orthogonal selective-prediction signal with
paired statistics and cross-architecture validation, not the act of
checking arithmetic itself. Program- and logic-level verification of
generative outputs has appeared in narrower settings (code-execution
validation, faithful chain-of-thought); $\sigma$ instantiates that
spirit for receipt KIE, where the target is closed-form arithmetic
over visible quantities.

We evaluate two architectures. Donut~\cite{kim2022donut} is a
\emph{generative} vision-encoder-decoder producing structured JSON;
LayoutLMv3~\cite{huang2022layoutlmv3} is an \emph{encoder-only}
token-classifier (tagging each token with a class such as
\texttt{Total\_value}). Their softmax confidences are
architecture-specific but conceptually identical: average per-token
probability on the predicted total-value tokens. Structure-aware
verification has appeared for table
reconstruction~\cite{nassar2022tableformer}; what is new here is
formalising an arithmetic check as a softmax-orthogonal selective
predictor on fixed off-the-shelf KIE models, with paired statistical
analysis across two architectures. Conformal
prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle} gives
calibrated coverage but no verifier-style precision decision rule; the two are
complementary and stackable (conformal calibrates from past
prediction-truth pairs; $\sigma$ verifies one prediction against the
document's own arithmetic).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Problem.} Given a receipt image $x$, a KIE model emits a
predicted total $\pi(x)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}$. Let $y(x)$ be the
gold total. A selective predictor is a binary decision rule
$g:x\mapsto\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}$ with
\emph{coverage} $C(g)=\Pr[g{=}\text{accept}]$ (how often it doesn't
defer) and \emph{precision}
$P(g)=\Pr(\pi{=}y\mid g{=}\text{accept})$ (how often it's right when
it doesn't defer).

\noindent\textbf{The $\sigma$ decision rule.} Let $M(x)=\{m_1,\dots,m_n\}$ be the
multiset of monetary amounts extracted from $x$. Fix
$k_{\min}\in\{1,2\}$ (the smallest subset size we'll accept as a
witness; without this, a single line item that happens to equal the
total would always pass) and tolerance $\varepsilon\geq 0$ (in
currency units). Define the reachable set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
T(M)=\Big\{\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}m_i\,\Big|\,
   S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},\,|S|\geq k_{\min}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ accepts $\pi$ iff $\inf_{t\in T(M)}|t-\pi|\leq\varepsilon$:
some subset of receipt amounts sums to the predicted total within
$\varepsilon$. $T(M)$ is computed by a textbook $0/1$-knapsack
DP. Amounts are stored as integer cents to avoid floating-point
surprises. Measured on CPU the per-receipt DP has median
\latMedian~\latUnit{} and $p_{99}$ of \latPNineNine~\latUnit{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}).

\noindent\textbf{Cardinality guard.} The right $k_{\min}$ depends on
whether the total typically appears on the receipt as a labelled
line. On labelled-amount corpora the labelled line is part of $M$ and
a singleton witness $\pi{=}m_j$ is a coincidence rather than the
intended structural match, so we set $k_{\min}{=}2$ to exclude it; on
OCR-derived corpora the OCR step often surfaces the labelled total
itself, and the singleton witness is then legitimate, so $k_{\min}{=}1$
helps on both axes (Table~\ref{tab:guard}). The default in this paper
is $k_{\min}{=}2$ on CORD and WildReceipt, $k_{\min}{=}1$ on SROIE.
This is selected without labels: $k_{\min}{=}1$ iff the extractor
surfaces a single amount equal to $\pi$ (within $\varepsilon$) on more
than a small fraction $\theta$ of receipts, an unlabelled, computable
property of the $(\pi, M)$ stream alone (we use $\theta{=}0.30$; the
three corpora separate cleanly at $0.07$/$0.05$/$0.41$ for
CORD/WildReceipt/SROIE, so the rule is insensitive to $\theta$ over
$[0.1,0.4]$). The guard is therefore a deployment-time decision rule,
not a label-tuned hyperparameter.

\noindent\textbf{Amount extraction $\tau$.} On labelled-amount corpora
(CORD), $M$ comes directly from the per-field JSON. On OCR-derived
corpora (SROIE), we use a multi-candidate extractor over the OCR text:
when a line is ambiguous (e.g.\ tax rate vs.\ tax amount), we keep
both interpretations as candidates $M_k$ and accept $\pi$ if any
candidate yields a witness ($3.12$ candidates per receipt on average).
On WildReceipt (encoder-only), $M$ comes from
\texttt{Prod\_price\_value} token annotations.

\noindent\textbf{Composition with softmax.} Let $s(x)$ be the softmax
confidence. For a coverage budget $c$, the softmax decision rule
accepts the top-$c$ fraction of receipts by $s$. We compose at
per-corpus matched coverage: $c$ equal to $\sigma$'s coverage on that
corpus. The combination of the two decision rules,
$\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax}$, accepts iff both fire.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Datasets.} We evaluate on three publicly available receipt datasets, pooled into a combined benchmark of 1,019 receipts:

•	CORD-v2 (200 receipts): A clean dataset where each field — including individual line item amounts — is precisely labelled. We use the test and validation splits only; the training split was used to fine-tune Donut and is excluded to prevent any overlap between training and evaluation data.
•	SROIE (347 receipts): A dataset where amounts are extracted from raw OCR text rather than clean labels, making it noisier and closer to real-world conditions.
•	WildReceipt (472 receipts): A dataset where amounts are identified by labelling each individual text token on the receipt, sitting between the other two in terms of label quality.


\noindent\textbf{AI Models.We test on two AI architectures, both used inference-only — meaning we do not retrain them, we simply run them on the evaluation data:

•	Donut: A generative model that reads a receipt image and produces structured JSON output. Used on CORD and SROIE.
•	LayoutLMv3: A classifier that labels each text token on the receipt with a category such as "total value." Used on WildReceipt.


\noindent\textbf{Settings.}We fix the tolerance at ε=0.02 (roughly two cents), meaning the arithmetic check accepts a predicted total if any subset of line items sums to within two cents of it. The minimum number of line items required to form that sum follows the cardinality guard rule described in Section III — at least two items on CORD and WildReceipt, and at least one on SROIE, selected automatically without using any labels.

\noindent\textbf{Statistics.} For every reported precision value we compute a Wilson 95\% confidence interval, which gives a statistically rigorous lower bound on the true precision rather than relying on a single point estimate that could be misleading on small samples. Agreement between σ and softmax is tested using the paired McNemar test.

\noindent\textbf{Softmax extraction across architectures.} For Donut
the softmax score is the geometric mean of per-token probabilities
over the predicted \texttt{total\_value} span in the decoder's
autoregressive output; for LayoutLMv3, the geometric mean of the
predicted-class probability on input tokens tagged
\texttt{Total\_value}. The two objects are architecture-specific
(decoder vs.\ token-classifier probabilities) but conceptually
analogous, each being the model's self-assessed probability of its
emitted total-value tokens, and we treat them as the
architecture-appropriate ``model self-confidence,'' not as
numerically interchangeable.

Table~\ref{tab:headline-pooled} is the pooled headline on all
$n{=}\poolN{}$ receipts. Reading the numbers in plain language: when
both decision rules agree to accept a receipt, the predicted total
is correct on $\poolIntC{}$ of $\poolIntA{}$ receipts, a Wilson
95\,\% lower bound of $\pcPoolWilson{}$, which clears the $0.95$
operationally meaningful floor for ``ship without a human in the
loop''. Each decision rule used on its own falls below that floor:
$\sigma$ alone has a lower bound near $0.918$ and softmax alone near
$0.927$. The lift comes at a coverage cost. The combination fires on
$18.1\%$ of receipts versus $38.1\%$ for either single rule, and on
SROIE only $4.3\%$ ($15/347$). The economics: with a human review
costing $\rho$ inference passes ($\rho\in[10^2,10^3]$,
Section~\ref{sec:intro}) and a silent wrong total costing $\kappa$,
automating at coverage $c$, precision $p$ on $N$ receipts costs
$cN(1{-}p)\kappa$ in undetected errors plus $(1{-}c)N\rho$ in reviews.
Moving from softmax-matched ($c{=}0.381$, $p{=}0.954$) to the
combination ($c{=}0.181$, $p{=}0.989$) trades $0.200N\rho$ extra
review for $(0.381{\cdot}0.046-0.181{\cdot}0.011)N\kappa
\approx 0.0155N\kappa$ fewer silent errors: favourable whenever
$\kappa\gtrsim 13\rho$, i.e.\ when one filed wrong total is worth more
than roughly a dozen human reviews, which holds in tax/audit
pipelines but not in low-stakes bulk capture: a condition, not a
universal claim. Table~\ref{tab:headline-percorpus} shows the lift is not a pooling
artefact, with each corpus's combined-rule precision above either
single rule (WildReceipt $113/114$, Wilson LB $\rgWrWil{}$; CORD
$54/55$, LB $\rgCordWil{}$; SROIE $15/15$, LB $\rgSroieWil{}$, where
$n{=}15$ supports only a consistency statement). The pooled
$n{=}\poolN{}$ is unevenly weighted at $200/347/472$ and carried by
WildReceipt, so the per-corpus table, not the pool, is the headline.
Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} gives the per-corpus four-cell matrix and
Fig.~\ref{fig:wildreceipt} singles out the WildReceipt case.

McNemar asks: when the two decision rules disagree, do they do so
asymmetrically? A small $p$ would mean one rule strictly dominates
the other on \emph{which} examples it gets right, weakening the case
for combining them. Non-rejection on every corpus is consistent with
the two rules being similarly accurate on disjoint slices; the
WildReceipt cell, $b{=}c{=}91$ exactly, is the cleanest example:
equal-size disjoint correct-accept slices that combine into the
$113/114{=}0.991$ combined-rule precision
(Fig.~\ref{fig:wildreceipt}). McNemar
non-rejection is, strictly, absence of evidence for asymmetry rather
than positive evidence of error-decorrelation; the direct
correlation-on-errors test (Section~\ref{sec:results-control})
supplies the latter.

\subsection{The Performance Gain is Genuine, Not a Statistical Coincidence}
\label{sec:results-control}

Combining any two better-than-random decision rules with imperfectly
correlated errors raises precision and lowers coverage; is the
headline just that generic effect? We re-analyse the same per-receipt
accept/correct vectors (no inference). Define the lift
$L=P(\sigma\sqcap\text{softmax})-P(\text{softmax at matched
coverage})$ and compare it, at identical coverage, against two
controls: (A)~a \emph{random} decision rule accepting a uniform
random subset of size $|\sigma{=}1|$ (no information; pure
coverage-shrink), and (B)~a \emph{correlated} decision rule
$\text{softmax}'=\text{softmax}+\text{noise}$, noise-calibrated so
$\text{softmax}'$ has $\sigma$-equal standalone precision (a
softmax-built, hence non-independent, decision rule of equal
quality). We test
the difference \emph{directly} with a paired bootstrap ($B{=}4000$):
each resample recomputes $L_\sigma$ and both control lifts on the
\emph{same} resample and takes the paired difference (non-overlap of
separately bootstrapped CIs is over-conservative and is not the test).
Pooled, $\sigma$'s lift exceeds the random decision rule by
$\orthDrand$ (95\,\% CI $[\orthDrandLo,\orthDrandHi]$, one-sided
$p{=}\orthDrandP$) and the correlated softmax-derived decision rule by
$\orthDcorr$
($[\orthDcorrLo,\orthDcorrHi]$, $p{=}\orthDcorrP$): significant against
\emph{both}. As a confound-free check we also measure error
decorrelation directly, within the softmax-accepted set: whether
$\sigma$ rejects precisely the softmax-wrong receipts, which gives
$\phi{=}\orthPhi$ (permutation $p{=}\orthPhiP$): positive evidence of
orthogonality with no coverage-shrink or combination-arithmetic
confound, which the paired-McNemar absence of asymmetry
(Section~\ref{sec:results-mcnemar}) only implies indirectly.
The pooled verdict is \orthEarned, but two honest caveats bound it.
First, the pooled effect is \emph{small} ($\orthDrand$/$\orthDcorr$
precision lift). The CORD evaluation is restricted to its
test+validation split ($n{=}200$); CORD-v2 \emph{train} is the Donut
fine-tuning data and is excluded, so no result here is inflated by
training-set leakage. Second, it does \emph{not} hold per corpus: an
exact-binomial test clears the $0.95$ floor individually only on
WildReceipt: CORD ($54/55$) and SROIE ($15/15$) are consistent with
$>0.95$ but underpowered and do \emph{not} individually pass
(F2d below, Section~\ref{sec:results-percorpus-rigor}). We therefore
claim a \emph{small, pooled, statistically-significant} orthogonality
and explicitly not a per-corpus or large one.

\smallskip
\label{sec:results-percorpus-rigor}
\noindent\textbf{Per-corpus rigor (Clopper--Pearson, LOCO, exact
TOST).} Exact small-sample analysis of the leakage-free
combination cells
(CORD $54/55$, SROIE $15/15$, WildReceipt $113/114$), pure stdlib (no
SciPy/PyMC). Lower bounds agree across estimators: CORD
Clopper--Pearson/Jeffreys/Wilson $\rgCordCPlo$/$\rgCordJeff$/$\rgCordWil$,
SROIE $\rgSroieCPlo$/$\rgSroieJeff$/$\rgSroieWil$ (SROIE
Clopper--Pearson is exactly $0.05^{1/15}$), WildReceipt
$\rgWrCPlo$/$\rgWrJeff$/$\rgWrWil$. Receipt-weighted pool $182/184$
($\hat p=\rgPoolPhat$, Wilson LB $\rgPoolWil$, Clopper--Pearson LB
$\rgPoolCPlo$); equal-corpus mean $\rgEqualMean$; leave-one-corpus-out
worst Wilson LB $\rgLocoMinWil$. A one-sided exact-binomial TOST
(H$_0$: $p\le0.95$, $\alpha{=}0.05$) clears the floor
\emph{individually only on WildReceipt} ($p=\rgTostWrP$); CORD
($p=\rgTostCordP$) and SROIE ($p=\rgTostSroieP$) do \emph{not} pass
(consistent with $>0.95$ but underpowered, stated plainly, not
softened), while the pool does ($p=\rgTostPoolP$). Monte-Carlo required-$n$
for $0.8$ power: $\rgReqnCord$/$\rgReqnSroie$/$\rgReqnWr$
(CORD/SROIE/WildReceipt), so CORD and SROIE are simply below it.
Per-receipt vectors \emph{are} present in the artifacts: a
within-corpus permutation test of $\sigma$'s orthogonal contribution,
combined across corpora, gives Stouffer $p=\rgAFiveStouffer$ (weights
$\sqrt{n_c}$) and Fisher $p=\rgAFiveFisher$: decorrelation holds
within every corpus, not only pooled. Honest reading: the
orthogonality is robust; the $0.95$ ship-without-review floor is met
individually only where the sample is large enough to show it.

\subsection{Practical Value: The Combined Rule Reduces Risk at Matched Coverage}
\label{sec:results-riskcov}

Statistical orthogonality is necessary but not sufficient for
deployment value; does the $\sigma$ decision rule beat simply
thresholding softmax? We answer with a matched-coverage risk--coverage comparison
(no inference): at each coverage, softmax-alone versus the $\sigma$
decision rule at the \emph{same} achieved coverage, integrated to an
AURC~\cite{geifman2019aurc} (lower better). Pooled, AURC is
$\rcAURCsoft$ (softmax-alone) versus $\rcAURCsig$ ($\sigma$ decision
rule), a paired-bootstrap difference of $\rcAURCd$ (95\,\% CI
$[\rcAURCdLo,\rcAURCdHi]$, one-sided $p{=}\rcAURCp$): the interval
excludes zero, so at matched coverage the $\sigma$ decision rule
significantly lowers AURC; verdict \rcEarned. We do not over-read
this: the absolute difference is small
($\rcAURCsoft\!\to\!\rcAURCsig$) and the lift costs coverage, so we
report it as a modest, statistically resolved operational gain, not a
large one, and it is concentrated where base error is non-trivial
(WildReceipt, the one regime with appreciable error, is where the
$\sigma$ decision rule is individually favourable). It is independent of, and
consistent with, the paper's central claim: \emph{statistical
softmax-orthogonality}, established directly at high significance in
Section~\ref{sec:results-control} (error decorrelation $\phi$,
permutation $p{=}\orthPhiP$, clearing a random and a
correlated-softmax control), plus an independent document-side audit
signal.

\subsection{When and Why Each Signal Performs Better}
\label{sec:results-regime}

Single-rule $\sigma$ precision is regime-mechanistic: on CORD (clean
per-field labels) $\sigma$ beats softmax by $3.0$ points ($0.990$
vs.\ $0.960$ at matched coverage); on WildReceipt (per-token
annotations) the two decision rules tie at $0.953$; on SROIE
(OCR-derived $M$) $\sigma$ trails ($0.863$ vs.\ $0.945$). We
hypothesise the SROIE loss is consistent with upstream OCR rather
than the verifier: digit confusions and phantom amounts can inflate
$T(M)$ with coincidental witnesses. This remains a hypothesis; the
controlled hand-OCR-corrected SROIE subsample test remains future
work. At matched coverage the combination of decision rules rises
above both single rules on WildReceipt and SROIE, and above the
matched-coverage softmax decision rule on CORD.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Where σ works best and where it struggles. The arithmetic check performs best when the line item amounts on the receipt are cleanly labelled — as in CORD — where it beats softmax by 3.0 precision points at matched coverage. On WildReceipt, where amounts come from per-token annotations, the two signals tie at 0.953 as standalone gates but reach 0.991 in combination. The sole regime where σ trails softmax as a standalone signal is SROIE, where amounts are extracted from raw OCR text. In this setting, digit confusions and phantom amounts introduced by the OCR process can accidentally sum to the predicted total, producing false witnesses. This remains a hypothesis; a controlled test on hand-corrected SROIE receipts is left for future work.
Why combining the two signals works. The intersection gain does not require either signal to dominate the other — it only requires them to fail on different receipts. WildReceipt illustrates this cleanly: both signals achieve identical 0.953 standalone precision yet fail on completely different receipts, so their combination rises to 0.991. This independence holds across both AI architectures tested — Donut and LayoutLMv3 — confirming that σ is orthogonal to softmax in general, not just for one specific model.
Operational guidance. The combined rule is most valuable in high-stakes settings such as tax filings and financial audits, where the cost of a silently wrong total propagating unchallenged outweighs the cost of additional human review. In low-stakes bulk capture, where speed and coverage matter more than precision, the coverage reduction from the intersection may not be justified.
What σ cannot do. The arithmetic check only works for fields that are sums of other visible amounts on the receipt — totals, subtotals, and taxes. It cannot verify fields like vendor name, date, or address. We evaluate a single field across two architectures and three datasets; extending σ to multiple fields simultaneously is left for future work. Additionally, σ requires the predicted total to be a clean numeric value. Outputs that include currency symbols, formatting noise, or post-hoc annotations require an upstream normalisation step before the check can be applied, and we do not benchmark that normalisation here.
Statistical scope. The 0.95 precision floor is cleared individually only on WildReceipt, which is large enough to provide sufficient statistical power. CORD (54/55) and SROIE (15/15) are consistent with exceeding 0.95 but their sample sizes are too small to confirm it individually. The pooled result (182/184) does clear the floor, but it is carried predominantly by WildReceipt (114 of 184 intersection accepts) and should be read as a pooled finding, not a per-dataset one. The SROIE intersection in particular — with only 15 accepted receipts — supports consistency with the overall finding but nothing stronger.
Scope of novelty. Arithmetic cross-checks on receipt totals are long-standing engineering practice in expense management software. The contribution of this paper is not the idea of checking arithmetic, but its formalisation as a statistically validated, softmax-orthogonal selective prediction signal with paired controls and cross-architecture evaluation. This is an applied contribution; no methodological novelty is claimed for the verifier itself.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we presented the sigma-verifier, a model-agnostic validation layer that directly evaluates a document by checking whether any subset of extracted itemized lines sums to the predicted total. We evaluated its performance across two document AI architectures, Donut and LayoutLMv3, on three receipt databases: CORD, SROIE, and WildReceipt.

Our results show that combining the sigma-verifier with the model's softmax confidence substantially reduces the silent-wrong-total rate among the receipts it accepts. On CORD the rate drops from 4.0\% to 1.8\% (a 55\% relative reduction); on WildReceipt from 4.7\% to 0.9\% ($\approx$81\%); on SROIE from 5.5\% to 0.0\% (n = 15, consistency only). Pooled across the three corpora it falls from 4.7\% to 1.1\%, an $\approx$77\% relative reduction, roughly four-fold fewer audit-exposing failures, at the cost of lower coverage: the combination accepts about 18\% of receipts versus about 38\% for either decision rule alone, and the pooled lift is individually significant only on WildReceipt. Used as an independent validation layer under that coverage trade, the sigma-verifier offers structural benefits for high-stakes financial reporting and downstream auditing systems.
